# S06: occupied Boolean cosets versus the actual observation

The useful distinction is between public sampler randomness and a secret-dependent precursor. Hiding sampler coins cannot repair information-theoretic lossiness under the stated independence assumptions; avoiding an unnecessarily informative precursor potentially can.

**Ledger.** The target retains growing power-of-two degree n, module rank one, odd polynomial q, coefficient basis, independent uniform Boolean coefficients, m independent ring samples, and independent coefficient discrete-Gaussian errors of width sigma. Toy lattice observations below explicitly change what is disclosed. No rotations count as independent samples; no experiments were run.

**Mechanism 1 — occupied cosets (fully written small calculation).** Let L be a fixed full-rank integer lattice and N_occ the number of its cosets meeting the Boolean cube. Each occupied fiber contributes exactly 2^(-n) to average guessing, so

    Htilde_infty(S | S mod L) = n-log2 N_occ.

The first change from the old argument is to count the partition of the actual cube, rather than the ambient index. However, N_occ=2^n exactly when L intersects {-1,0,1}^n only at zero. For L=(f), either sigma_min(M_f)>sqrt(n) or |Norm(f)|>n^(n/2) certifies this injection; the latter follows from norm divisibility and Hadamard. Scalar f=K>=2 already has maximal occupancy despite an arbitrarily large index. Conversely, f=1+X gives only two occupied cosets, since R/(1+X)=F_2. This attractive geometry supplies no pseudorandom sampler.

For the explicit **unwrapped** augmentation (V=S+G,C=G mod L), conditional support counting improves the leakage bound to log2 N_occ. It also shows that maximal occupancy makes this augmentation determine S exactly. The serious objection is therefore substantive cube geometry, not merely a loose determinant estimate. A cheap next test is to apply the two injection certificates before enumerating small-degree cosets.

**Normalization qualification.** BD's Lemma 6.5 restates an inverse estimate using canonical norms, while the cited original uses coefficient norms. Its probability transfer remains unchecked here. Independently, the original small-ball proof supports a conservative certificate: for coefficient Gaussian width kappa, delta=1/4, t=16n, kappa>=t eta_delta(Z^n)/sqrt(2pi), and kappa>16sqrt(2)n, cube injection holds with probability greater than one half before conditioning on invertibility. Canonical width is gamma=sqrt(n)kappa. Conditioning on mod-q invertibility needs its own probability bound. These are deductions from the explicitly normalized projection bound, not a claim covering arbitrary q or every old parameter choice. [Stehlé–Steinfeld, Section 2.2 and Lemma 4.1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2013/004.pdf).

**Mechanism 2 — actual likelihood (fully written small calculation).** If A=F(T) and (S,E) is jointly independent of T with a fixed error law, Bayes' formula gives

    Pr[S=s | A,Y,T] = Pr[S=s | A,Y],  Y=AS+E mod q.

Thus revealing T changes neither posterior guessing nor average conditional min-entropy. It may change computational convenience. A secret-dependent precursor U need not obey this equality: even the scalar toy U=S, Y=U+E loses information because wrapped Gaussian errors have full support. A simulation from U to Y only upper-bounds transcript guessing by precursor guessing.

For the actual ring transcript, the precise object is

    p_guess(S | A=a,Y)
      = 2^(-n) sum_y max_s product_{i,j}
          W_{sigma,q}(y_{i,j}-(M_{a_i}s)_j).

The first substantive change is to bound this wrapped likelihood directly. When f is invertible modulo q, (f)+qR=R: modular reduction does not automatically disclose an integer lattice coset. Any approximate disclosure needs a full no-wrap estimate.

**Unresolved implication.** Either mechanism still needs a public-A sampler indistinguishable from uniform and actual transcript lossiness on a nonnegligible fraction of A. BD's Definition 4.1/Theorem 4.2 then give mild search hardness; unchanged Gaussian errors, sample amplification, and decision hardness need additional work. The next cheap diagnostic is a small fixed-matrix comparison of actual and precursor guessing, with controlled wrapped-Gaussian tails. [Brakerski–Döttling, Sections 4–5](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1185.pdf).
