Let T be the signed cyclic shift, M_s=sum_i s_i T^i, and use the Euclidean coefficient norm throughout. Since (Delta,Z) has one common joint law independent of S, its RAW second moments C=E Delta Delta^T, B=E ZZ^T and L=E Z Delta^T exist. In particular, tr C=E||Delta||², not a centered variance. Dependence between Delta and Z is allowed. Expanding W_s gives V_s=B-LM_s^T-M_sL^T+M_sCM_s^T. All these are second moments, and no mean-zero premise has been supplied or used.

On the four secrets U+e0+e1,U+e0+e3,U+e2+e1,U+e2+e3, taking signs +,-,-,+ cancels the constant and every linear term, including those containing the arbitrary baseline U. The remaining quadratic mixed term is D=P C(TP)^T+(TP)CP^T for P=I-T². This identity holds entrywise and does not require independent components, Gaussianity, an isotropic C, or random choices of U.

T is real orthogonal and has a unitary eigenbasis with eigenvalues exp(i theta_j), theta_j=(2j+1)pi/n. If c_j is the diagonal of C in that basis, c_j>=0 and sum c_j=tr C. The jth diagonal of D equals 2 Re(exp(i theta_j)) |1-exp(2i theta_j)|² c_j=8 sin²(theta_j)cos(theta_j)c_j. For a power-of-two n>=4, both the nearest distance to a multiple of pi and the nearest distance to pi/2 modpi are at least pi/n. Hence |sin theta_j|,|cos theta_j|>=sin(pi/n)>=2/n. If |cos theta_j|>=1/2 the coefficient magnitude is >=16/n². Otherwise sin² theta_j>3/4 gives magnitude >12/n>=16/n². This verifies the constant, including n4. Therefore

    ||D||_F >= (16/n²)(sum_j c_j²)^(1/2)
              >=16 tr(C)/n^(5/2),
    ||D||_op >= ||D||_F/sqrt(n) >=16 tr(C)/n³.

The second inequality is a lower bound on the largest singular value from Frobenius norm, not its reverse. D is real symmetric. For any common Y with V_*=E YY^T, each face has sum_s ||V_s-V_*||_op>=||D||_op. Exactly 2^(n-4) disjoint faces lie in the full cube; they cover one quarter of its points. Dividing their total by 2^n gives average d_s>=tr(C)/n³. On a weight-w slice there are binom(n-4,w-2) disjoint faces. The ratio of that number to binom(n,w) is

    w(w-1)(n-w)(n-w-1)/(n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3))=p_w/4.

Multiplying by16 gives the stated4p_w coefficient. At n4,w2, p_w=2/3, so the slice coefficient is8/3; the counting still gives only one four-point face among six slice points. Neither all cube points nor all slice points are falsely claimed to lie in those faces.

For each s, choose a real unit eigenvector u_s of the symmetric difference V_s-V_* attaining its operator norm in absolute value. On the bounded supports, f_s(x)=(u_s^T x)²/R² lies in[0,1]. Thus the difference of its expectations, in absolute value, is d_s/R² and is bounded ABOVE by TV; the resulting lower bound has no extra factor1/2. A globally bounded measurable extension of f_s can be taken by clipping it at1. The test is allowed to depend on the retained secret s. Since S has exactly the same finite marginal in both compared experiments, joint TV is exactly the mean of these fixed-s distances. This step cannot be projected in reverse to infer a lower bound after discarding S.

For finite second moments without bounded support, use the clipped test min((u_s^T x)²,R²)/R². The difference between its scaled expectation and the raw quadratic expectation is at most E[||x||² 1_{||x||>R}]/R² for each endpoint. Triangle inequality and averaging give precisely the stated eta_S and eta_Y penalties.
