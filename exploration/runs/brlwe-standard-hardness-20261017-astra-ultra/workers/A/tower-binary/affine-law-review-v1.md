I checked the entire argument. Equality of the pushforward distribution and uniform cube distribution gives equality of their supports. A row containing two nonzero entries a,b yields at least three output values in odd characteristic: b distinct from a,-a makes 0,a,b distinct; b=a yields 0,a,2a; b=-a yields -a,0,a. Thus a row has at most one nonzero coefficient. Invertibility gives one per row with distinct columns. Mapping the two inputs to the two values 0,1 forces coefficient and offset (1,0) or (-1,1). The converse is a coordinate permutation with optional bit complements.

For negacyclic multiplication, the first column is the coefficient vector of the multiplying element. A signed permutation multiplication matrix therefore forces u=+/-Y^j. Each such u is a unit and does give a signed coordinate permutation. The correcting affine offset is unique. There are exactly 2N distinct such u for odd q. For a fixed A, the resulting orbit has at most 2N points, so its distance from the uniform q^N-point distribution is at least 1-2N/q^N. If that bound is negative it is simply vacuous, not false. The support bound does not claim that arbitrary additional public or secret-dependent operations are covered.

The finite falsification control A-tower-finite-1 independently enumerated all 432 invertible affine maps of F_3^2 and all 12,000 invertible affine maps of F_5^2. Exactly eight cube-preserving maps occurred in each field, all of the stated form. Separate exhaustive negacyclic-unit/offset enumeration in degree two found exactly the four multipliers +/-1,+/-Y in both fields. A characteristic-two negative control [[1,1],[0,1]] preserves the full binary cube while failing to be a signed permutation, confirming that the odd-characteristic restriction matters. Complete outputs and resource data are retained in finite-stdout-v1.json and finite-resources-v1.txt.

These finite checks support falsification only; the preceding algebra is the proof review. The verdict does not extend to approximate law preservation, nonlinear maps, oracle-assisted programming, secret-dependent conditioning, or any unproved response completion. In particular a random unit can alter the binary-secret law, but this lemma alone does not preclude a different reduction that proves a new near-uniform binary coupling.
