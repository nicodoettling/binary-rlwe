# Testing modular wraparound as an escape from the lifting condition

Provisional direction test, not a theorem or a hardness conclusion. The full/source reviewed information-retention statement has a strict large-q premise. This note tests one proposed escape outside that premise without broadening its conclusion.

The exact model for the numerical test is degree2, R=Z[i], J=mR, m coprime q. Planted a=u/m where the two coefficients of u are independent D_(Z,ms); A=m^-1 u modulo q. The comparator is uniform A in R/qR. In both worlds S has two independent uniform binary coefficients and E has independent D_(Z,sigma) coefficients, B=A S+E modulo q. Thus the entire actual-secret response channel is retained. J is binary-injective for every tested m>=2. This principal family is easily sampled and supplies no standard worst-case family transfer.

For each A, the optimal success is calculated as one quarter of the sum over all B of the maximum of the four wrapped-error likelihoods. Averaging that same function under planted A and uniform A gives both endpoints. The source and error laws use their actual periodized Gaussian sums, evaluated with a finite cutoff and numerical normalization; they are not replaced by uniform cosets or matching covariance. The analytic omitted-tail bound is recorded in the code/output, but floating-point summation has not been interval-certified. These are numerical observations only.

The large observed gaps occur chiefly at narrow source width or the small denominator2 with one source sample. At q37,m2,s0.5,sigma1, planted optimal success is about0.36434 versus uniform-comparator0.99876. This does not challenge the information theorem: its source-coset flatness and denominator-generation losses are retained and can be large. At s>=1 and m*sigma>=q, the largest observed comparator-minus-planted gap in the grid is about0.01109; this does not exclude larger gaps elsewhere or in higher degree. One-sample degree2 data cannot justify an asymptotic conclusion for polynomially many samples.

One proposed shortcut is false for an exact algebraic reason: multiplication of an integer error residue by a unit m modulo q is a permutation. Hence TV(law(mE modq),uniform)=TV(law(E modq),uniform), in one coordinate and jointly. Making the real magnitude m*sigma exceed q does not by itself flatten the discrete residue law. Every tested case also checks this equality numerically. A continuous-Gaussian smoothing argument that ignores the changed supporting lattice would apply to a different distribution.

The parameter gap in the full theorem remains open. This test provides no source-hardness edge, no general information-retention theorem outside the lifting regime, no independent-mask construction, and no extension of the accepted integral A/B/C chain to a fractional ideal. The next constructive bridge still needs a complete joint-law argument and a useful worst-case family transfer.
