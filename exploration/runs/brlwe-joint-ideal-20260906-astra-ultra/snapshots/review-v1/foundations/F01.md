## 1. Exact probability space and public interface

Use the frozen packet's notation. For the fully split application write R as the CRT product F_q^n. An ideal J vanishes on a coordinate set T, its quotient Q_J consists of the T coordinates, and I_J=Ann(J) is supported on T. Put d_J=|T|. If U is nonzero at every coordinate of T and zero elsewhere, multiplication by U is the required bijection Q_J -> I_J. The definitions also cover d_J=0: both spaces have one element and U=0 gives a bijection.

Let p_S,p_G,p_E be the fixed secret, auxiliary, and target error mass functions, and let mu(j,u,f,h) be the exact source law. A single encompassing probability space, before forgetting different coordinates at different proof steps, has mass

    mu(j,u,f,h) / |I_j| * p_S(s) * p_G(g) * p_E(e)

at (j,u,f,h,v,s,g,e), for v in I_j. Thus (S,G,E) are mutually independent and independent of the entire source tuple; V is independent of (U,F,H) given J. The ideal, U, F, and H may otherwise be arbitrarily correlated. Y=S+G is derived, not independently resampled after a conditioning event. On the extraction-replaced branch, adjoin Z uniformly in Q_J with independent fresh randomness conditional on J.

The input to the computational reduction is only (a,c). It receives neither source coins nor J,U,F,H,V. B1 chooses its own secret and target error and ignores c. B2 samples its own fresh s0,g0 and computes y=s0+g0 before using (a,c). The variables s0,g0 are computational sampling coins for the distribution of Y, not the latent secret S and G in the source-to-target derivation. This difference is legitimate because the final source/Y representation proves their requisite independence as distributions.

The source challenger must define a sampler without a binary source secret. The theorem needs an efficient source experiment for a conventional computational assumption, but neither reduction runs that sampler or computes its hidden objects. An existential completion coupling alone does not specify such a source sampler. The exact law of (J,U,F,H) used in all premises must match the exact paired challenge whose hardness is assumed.

## 2. Elementary probability facts used

All spaces here are finite after reduction modulo q. We use the following elementary identities; no imported research theorem is load-bearing.

**L1: common-marginal conditional TV.** If P(W)=Q(W)=p, then

    TV(P,Q) = sum_w p(w) TV(P(X|w),Q(X|w)).

This follows by factoring p(w) in the defining absolute-value sum. Zero-mass fibers contribute zero and need no conditional definition. An average eta does not imply an eta bound for every w. Markov's inequality only gives bad-fiber probability at most eta/t for conditional distance exceeding t.

**L2: shared kernels and tests.** For a common randomized kernel K, TV(PK,QK)<=TV(P,Q), by the triangle inequality inside the output sum and sum_y K(y|x)=1. Appending an independent variable with the same law preserves TV exactly. Every bit-valued randomized test has expectation discrepancy at most TV: condition on its random tape or use its acceptance function in [0,1]. The constant is one with our TV convention; the factor two relevant to arbitrary complex functions of modulus one is not used here.

**L3: extraction auxiliaries.** Suppose extraction is first established for (J,Y,pi_J(S)) and a conditionally independent uniform quotient variable. Independence of Gamma from (S,G) allows us to append (U,F,H) using the kernel mu(u,f,h|j). The augmented joint TV is exactly the base TV, since the original coordinates are retained and this conditional mass sums to one. This explains why extraction jointly with J extends to the specified source auxiliaries without loss. It does not apply if H or the source coins encode the same G used in Y.

**L4: affine image of quotient uniformity.** For fixed j,u,y with u in I_j and K=Z-pi_j(y), K is uniform in Q_j. The map Q_j -> uR has equally sized fibers (cosets of its kernel), so uK is uniform in uR. If m_u is bijective, this is uniform in I_j. For a subgroup L of a finite additive group I,

    TV(Unif(L),Unif(I)) = 1-|L|/|I|.

The equality follows by summing separately over L and I\L. When the retained variables determine L, L1 averages this identity exactly.

These four facts are sufficient for the statistical part of the proof. The finite regressions below test the conditions under which they are applied.

## 3. Full hybrid proof

For each public pair distribution H_i, let p_i be D's acceptance probability, with D's independent coins included. The hybrid labels below name only the public pair; different proof edges may retain different latent coordinates. There is no claim that one maximal latent transcript is close at every edge.

    H0: (a,aS+E), where a is uniform and independent.

    H1: (U+F,(U+F)S+E).

    H2: (U+F,US+F(S+G)+H) = (U+F,US+FY+H).

    H3: (U+F,U Z+F Y+H), with Z uniform in Q_J.

    H4: (a,b), with a,b independently uniform in R.

Here UZ means m_U(Z), not multiplication of U by a chosen unknown representative. It is well-defined because UJ=0. Each occurrence of source variables has law mu, and H2 uses exactly the same independent S,G prescribed in the hypotheses.

**H0 to H1: the first source test.** Under a uniform source challenge, B1 sees a uniform first coordinate and produces H0. Under Src_mu it sees U+F and produces H1. The independently chosen S,E have the target laws in both experiments. Hence

    |p_1-p_0| = Adv_src(B1).

This uses the first-coordinate marginal of the exact paired source via an adversary that discards its second input. No separate source marginal hypothesis is needed.

**H1 to H2: noise comparison without H in the retained tuple.** Premise (N) compares (J,U,F,E) with (J,U,F,FG+H). Append an independent S, preserving TV, then apply

    (j,u,f,s,n) -> (u+f,(u+f)*s+n).

The first branch is H1. The second branch is H2 because FS+FG=F(S+G). Thus |p_1-p_2|<=eta_noise. The map depends only on variables retained in (N) plus independent S. It neither reads H separately nor needs to append Y to this comparison. In particular, (N) need not hold after exposing H, G, or the pair (S,Y). On the H2 side those variables can be introduced according to their original generating law for the next edge.

**H2 to H3: extraction with H retained.** Apply (X) to the common map

    (j,u,f,h,y,z) -> (u+f,m_u(z)+f*y+h).

Its real branch has z=pi_j(S), hence m_u(z)=US and is H2. Its uniform branch is H3. This map reads H; extraction that omits H cannot justify this edge. Consequently |p_2-p_3|<=eta_ext.

**H3 is a public transform of the source.** Define K=Z-pi_J(Y) and V'=UK. Then

    UZ+FY+H = (U+F)Y+V'+H.

For every positive-mass retained tuple (j,u,f,h,y), bijectivity and L4 give

    Pr[V'=v | j,u,f,h,y] = 1/|I_j|, for v in I_j.

In particular V' is conditionally independent of (U,F,H,Y) given J. This conclusion is derived after replacing the quotient variable; it is not an assumption about U*pi_J(S). Since Y was sampled independently of Gamma, the full joint law is

    mu(j,u,f,h) * Pr[Y=y] / |I_j|.

This equals the law of (Gamma,Y,V) in the independent source/Y experiment. Thus H3 equals the output of B2's public map under Src_mu.

For nonbijective U, the same factorization holds with V' uniform in UR instead of I_J. By L1 and L4, its joint TV from the source/Y law is exactly

    eta_iso = E_mu[1-|UR|/|I_J|].

The public H3/source-map distance is at most eta_iso by L2. No rejection or conditioning is necessary for this version.

**Source transform to H4: the second source test.** For each fixed y, the map (a,c)->(a,ay+c) is a bijection of R^2, with inverse (a,b)->(a,b-ay). It takes Unif_2 to Unif_2, even when a or y is a zero divisor. Therefore B2(Unif_2) has D-acceptance p_4, and in the bijective case

    |p_3-p_4|=Adv_src(B2).

In the general case the right side acquires at most eta_iso.

The signed telescoping identity is

    p_0-p_4 = -(p_1-p_0)+(p_1-p_2)+(p_2-p_3)+(p_3-p_4).

Taking absolute values proves (T1), with +eta_iso in the general case. The two computational gaps concern B1 and B2 separately. They cannot generally be identified, canceled, or replaced by the maximum without a factor two. Every D supplies both tests with the stated one-call overhead. No adaptive selection, amplification, or repeated source challenge is used.

## 4. Source law, active invertibility, and implementation losses

For uniform U in an ideal with d active CRT coordinates, those coordinates are independent uniform field elements. Consequently

    p_good = (1-1/q)^d,
    kappa = 1-(1-1/q)^d.

If exactly z active coordinates vanish, |UR|/|I|=q^(-z). Averaging independent coordinates gives the sharper exact penalty

    eta_iso = 1-(1-1/q+1/q^2)^d,
    (1-1/q)*kappa <= eta_iso <= kappa.

For q>=2n+1 and 1<=d<=n, kappa>=d/(2q). With q polynomially bounded this is not negligible. It is an explicit finite loss that can be adequate for an advantage larger than it, but it cannot be silently omitted from a negligible-error theorem.

Sampling U uniformly with nonzero active coordinates removes eta_iso and changes the source. Given J, draw each active coordinate uniformly in F_q^*, set the others to zero, and invert the CRT. This is efficient when the source sampler knows its CRT support. Sampling nonzero coordinates directly avoids a whole-tuple rejection. If F,H depend on U, their prescribed conditional kernel must also be respected; retaining a previously sampled F,H after replacing U defines yet another law.

For a fixed J, conditioning uniform U on goodness changes its latent law by exactly kappa(J). If the J law is preserved and U is conditioned separately for each J, the average latent change is E_J kappa(J); public source TV is at most that value. Conditioning the entire tuple globally also changes the J marginal when p_good(J) varies, so these two conditioning procedures are not identical. They coincide in this respect when d is fixed and the conditional U law is uniform.

In the reference q>=2n+1 setting, whole-U rejection for fixed J has success probability >1/2 and expected fewer than two trials. Capping after L trials gives failure <2^(-L), with a specified failure output. This establishes implementability, not indistinguishability of the altered source. A computational assumption for unconditioned uniform ideal points does not automatically transfer with negligible loss through an inverse-polynomial change of distribution.

There are two clean bookkeeping choices:

1. Define the implemented source law itself as mu and prove its premises directly. The exact chosen U law, F/H correlation, truncations, precision, and any abort branch belong to that assumption.
2. Prove premises for an idealized mu and establish a pair TV error xi_src to the implemented source. Every use of the source endpoint is then charged. There are two such uses in this theorem.

For (T2), B1's ideal source-output probability differs from its executed counterpart by at most xi_src+xi_1, and its ideal uniform-output probability by at most xi_1. Thus its edge costs at most Adv_src_actual(B1_tilde)+xi_src+2xi_1. B2's source-output discrepancy is at most xi_src+xi_2. Its uniform-output discrepancy is exactly zero, for any independent approximate y law, because the public affine map preserves uniformity pointwise in y. Hence that edge costs Adv_src_actual(B2_tilde)+xi_src+xi_2. A target-law error adds xi_tgt. This proves the displayed implementation accounting.

If only a conditional noise bound is known off a bad source event of mass beta, the unconditional noise distance is at most (1-beta)*eta_cond+beta. The same statement applies separately to extraction when the conditioning event is measurable in the common retained variables and the stated conditional laws really are the ones used. Do not treat conditioned bounds as full-law bounds or claim a single universal bad-event charge for several changed source endpoints. The direct eta_iso calculation already pays its own map failure once; an additional kappa for that same failure would double count.

Exact target Gaussians may be treated as mathematical laws. For actual polynomial-time sampling, a polynomial-width sigma with an effective parameter description permits truncation and finite-precision categorical sampling, with a proved joint error xi_1. The theorem does not assume access to a sampler for an arbitrary uncomputable real sigma. Alternatively formulate the comparison against the exact finite law actually sampled. Approximate uniform source sampling, rejection failures, and completion precision similarly enter the specified source law or xi_src. No source sampler has been implemented or certified by this assignment.

## 5. Public correctness versus stronger latent premises

The proof only needs a public TV bound between H1 and H2, a public TV bound between H2 and H3, and the source-map identity at the next edge. Premises (N),(X) are sufficient certificates for those public facts. A direct proof of the same public facts may work when the latent premises fail. Consequently an obstruction to (N), or to extraction after revealing H, rules out this template under those premises; it does not rule out all public simulators.

Conversely, public computational pseudorandomness of the source cannot replace either statistical certificate without a new reduction. The source contains no S in its input definition, and its precise paired law is a separate hypothesis. Uniform-looking marginals alone do not imply the joint assumption.

The most consequential implementation trap is to choose fresh E,G and define H=E-FG, then regard this as both a source sampler and the desired coupling. If that same G is used in Y, the source tuple is correlated with G, violating the public-independent auxiliary interface. If the source privately uses G0 and B2 uses a fresh independent G, then the effective error is E+F(G-G0), whose target distribution must be proved anew. Hiding G0 from the adversary does not restore the required generative independence. The finite example in Section 7 makes this distinction exact.

## 6. What decision implies about recovery

The theorem's endpoint is decisional, but one-sample recovery can be related without pretending that a verifier identifies the original planted witness.

Let Rec use the one input sample. On exact planted success, s_hat=S and the verification residual is E. Therefore, with the residual set T in the frozen packet,

    Pr_planted[D_Rec accepts] >= epsilon_rec-delta_T.

Success and the error-tail event can be correlated because Rec saw that error through its input. Multiplying epsilon_rec by Pr[E in T] here would be unjustified.

For a uniform input (a,b), fix a. For each fixed binary s there are |T| choices of b with b-a*s in T. The union over all 2^n binary s therefore has at most 2^n |T| elements. Any adaptively chosen binary output s_hat can pass only in this union. Averaging over a proves

    Pr_uniform[D_Rec accepts] <= min(1,2^n |T|/|R|).

This is a union bound over all candidates, not an assumption that Rec's chosen candidate is independent of its input. It implies the recovery consequence in the frozen statement. It also explains why verification of an arbitrary planted-distribution preimage is not verification of equality to S: the latter is used solely to lower-bound acceptance.

For the baseline choose B=ceil(sigma log n), eventually B>=1, and let T be the coefficient box modulo q. Since

    (2B+1)/q <= 2*log(n)/n^c + 3/(2n+1),

eventually 2B+1<q and |T|=(2B+1)^n. Thus 2^n |T|/q^n=(2(2B+1)/q)^n is negligible. For a scalar unwrapped D_sigma sample Z, monotonicity and the Gaussian integral tail estimate give

    Pr[|Z|>B]
       <= 2/Z_sigma * integral_B^infinity exp(-pi*x^2/sigma^2) dx
       <= sigma^2/(pi*B*Z_sigma) * exp(-pi*B^2/sigma^2)
       <= sigma^2/(pi*B) * exp(-pi*log(n)^2).

Here Z_sigma>=1; no continuous approximation to the discrete law is made. A union bound over n independent coordinates gives delta_T at most n times this expression. Independence is unnecessary for this particular union bound, though the actual target is a product. Because sigma<=q<=n^K and B>=1, the bound is negligible. Modular wrapping can only reduce the event of falling outside the residue box. These estimates establish the nonempty residual-verification regime already contained in the frozen target; they do not establish a feasible source/coupling regime.

For a solver using m samples, a held-out verifier needs ell additional independent samples. Let W comprise the first m samples and the solver's tape, and let s_hat be W-measurable. Conditional on (W,S), the ell held-out errors are independent with their target law. On s_hat=S, simultaneous verification therefore has probability alpha_T^ell. This yields planted acceptance >=epsilon_rec alpha_T^ell. On a fully uniform challenge, condition on W, the candidate, and all held-out multipliers; the held-out response coordinates remain independent uniform elements, giving acceptance at most v_T^ell. This proves the held-out statement with a genuine conditional product calculation. If the recovery algorithm sees those samples before fixing s_hat, this proof does not apply.

A self-verifier using all m solver inputs has a different valid bound: planted acceptance >=epsilon_rec-m*delta_T and uniform acceptance <=2^n v_T^m, for iid target errors and independent uniform responses. This uses an ordinary fixed-candidate union bound and can be useful if an m-sample decision theorem is separately proved. Neither argument supplies m target samples from a one-sample theorem. Rotations, repeated added noise, or independent secrets do not fill that gap.

Finally, public transcript TV suffices for decision. Transferring exact planted recovery success between two implementations requires a comparison of their joint (S,transcript) laws, or a separate success-event argument. The recovery statement above is for the mathematical target law. The optional xi_tgt public-TV term in (T2) by itself does not assert closeness of planted witnesses in arbitrary alternative implementations.

## 7. Finite-support reasoning and mandatory regressions

These are exact hand calculations on finite spaces, not executed experiments or asymptotic hardness evidence. No CPU experiment allocation was requested or used. Each model identifies the premise being exercised or rejected.

**FR1 — complete finite one-sample check, with unequal source tests and a tight extraction charge.** Let R=F_3, J=0, U=1, F=H=E=0. Let S be uniform on {0,1}, G uniform on F_3, and V uniform on F_3. Then Y is uniform and independent of S; extraction TV is exactly 1/3, and noise TV is zero. Source A=1 and C is uniform. Let D accept the five pairs with b in {0,a}. Under the target it always accepts; under uniform pairs it accepts with probability 5/9, so Adv_tgt=4/9. B1 always produces b=a*s and accepts under either source experiment, hence Adv_src(B1)=0. B2 under the source has a=1 and b uniform, so accepts with probability 2/3; under uniform it accepts with probability 5/9, giving Adv_src(B2)=1/9. Thus 4/9=0+1/9+1/3. Direct enumeration of the three values of Z also gives V'=Z-Y uniform for every retained Y. This checks the algebra, the two distinct adversaries, the factor-one TV charge, and the conditional uniform-image step.

**FR2 — retaining H during noise comparison would be an unjustified stronger premise.** Work over F_2 with J=0,U=1,F=0 and independent fair S,G,H. Let E be fair. Then FG+H=H is uniform, so (N) has distance zero. Given H,Y, S is still fair because G is independent uniform, so extraction has distance zero. Nevertheless (H,FG+H) is supported on the diagonal whereas (H,E) is independent uniform, with TV=1/2. The actual theorem permits this example. Its proof never asks to retain H on the noise edge.

**FR3 — dropping H from extraction can fail.** For independent fair S,G over F_2 set Y=S+G and H=G. The pair (Y,S) is independent uniform, so marginal extraction has zero error. Given H,Y, however, S=Y-H deterministically. The retained joint extraction distance from an independent fair Z is 1/2. This model violates source/auxiliary independence and is correctly rejected by the frozen theorem. It is the finite version of revealing coins that encode G.

**FR4 — marginal-only noise can falsely prove a uniform source implies a nonuniform target is uniform.** Over F_2 choose J=0,U=1, fair independent F,S,G, and H=F. Let V be independently fair. Then A=1+F and C=V+F are jointly uniform; the source is exactly Unif_2. Y=S+G is uniform independent of S and of (F,H), so extraction error is zero. The effective noise N=FG+H=F(G+1) has mass 3/4 at zero. Set the target E to have exactly that marginal law. A noise comparison that forgot F would have error zero. But the true target has Pr[(a,b)=(0,0)]=3/8, while uniform pairs give 1/4: an efficient test has advantage 1/8. The correct retained comparison has TV=1/4: conditional on F=0 the noise is zero, and conditional on F=1 it is fair, each at distance 1/4 from E. Thus marginal noise equality is insufficient even when extraction and the paired source comparison are exact. This is a probability control outside the odd-q Gaussian baseline, not a claim about that baseline.

**FR5 — source marginals are insufficient.** Over F_3 let A be uniform and C=A. Both marginals are uniform, but the equality test accepts with probability one instead of 1/3. The pair TV is 2/3. The theorem assumes the whole pair; it does not treat this as a hard source.

**FR6 — active zero coordinates cost actual mass.** Over F_3 take I=R,J=0 and uniform U. The bad event U=0 has probability 1/3. For uniform Z, conditional U=0 the product UZ is zero, while an independent uniform V has distance 2/3. Conditional U nonzero the distance is zero. Hence the retained joint distance is (1/3)*(2/3)=2/9, agreeing with 1-(1-1/3+1/9). Replacing this by a zero loss is false. Conditioning U to {1,2} changes its law by 1/3.

**FR7 — mandatory shared-K multi-sample regression.** Over F_3 let J=0,U_1=U_2=1 and K uniform. Then V_1=V_2=K each has the correct uniform marginal. Their pair is diagonal and has TV 2/3 from two fresh uniform ideal points. More generally, conditional on fixed bijective U_1,...,U_m in an ideal of size N, the common-K vector ranges uniformly over N points inside I^m, giving distance 1-N^(1-m) from independent ideal points. This exact retained-law obstruction rules out silently reusing the one-sample image argument for independent points. A new public-only proof or a different joint source could be investigated separately by D03.

**FR8 — same-input success does not factor through error tails.** A two-atom probability space has a good-error event of probability 1/2 and a solver-success event equal to its complement, also of probability 1/2. Their intersection has probability zero, not 1/4. The self-verification bound uses epsilon_rec-delta_T=0, while the held-out product uses additional randomness independent of solver success. This model rejects the tempting epsilon_rec*alpha_T lower bound for verification on the solver's own input.

**FR9 — hiding completion coins does not make a fresh auxiliary reuse them.** Over F_3 fix F=1, E=0 and let a source secretly choose uniform G0 and H=-G0. If the bridge chooses independent uniform G, then FG+H=G-G0 is uniform and its TV from the target error zero is 2/3. If it instead reuses G=G0, the noise is perfect but H reveals G, so extraction after retaining H,Y reveals S. These are distinct generative experiments; calling both errors E is invalid.

**FR10 — conditioning average versus every fiber.** Let W be fair and set P(X|W=0)=Q(X|W=0), while P(X|W=1),Q(X|W=1) are different point masses. Joint TV is 1/2, but conditional TV on W=1 is one. All uses of (N),(X) retain averages; no proof step promotes them to uniform per-source-coin guarantees.

The imported Gaussian-correlation control R04 gives another multi-sample warning: errors E_1=Z and E_2=(-1)^(S xor T)Z may have individually correct Gaussian marginals while jointly leaking a bit relation. Nothing in this note upgrades individual noise comparisons to an iid multi-sample comparison. The prior planted-versus-valid-preimage example is also respected: self-verification recognizes a residual property, and the planted equality event is used only in a one-way lower bound.

The cached-prefix CP-1 regression is not a premise of any fixed-coordinate independence claim here. No adaptive selected-stage product is used. Its old inference is explicitly rejected: the reported probability .0105 exceeds .01 despite residual marginal bounds. The held-out product above is instead justified pointwise after conditioning on the complete prefix and candidate, with fresh independent responses; if fresh samples were replaced by cached or shared values, this conditioning assertion would fail and the product would be unavailable.
