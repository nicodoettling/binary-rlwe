For a full-rank integral column basis B, eR subset J is equivalent to eB^-1 integral. Thus the additive quotient exponent is exactly the lcm of all reduced denominators of B^-1. Exact rational inversion and lcm use polynomial binary work in n and the full supplied basis encoding. Under the promise e=p. This calculation does not factor e or prove its primality.

The cyclic group F_p^* has order p-1, divisible by2n. Raising b to(p-1)/(2n) maps it onto the order2n subgroup with equal fiber size(p-1)/(2n). Since2n is a power of two, half that subgroup has exact order2n, and alpha^n=-1 is equivalent to that exact order. Exactly(p-1)/2 of the nonzero field representatives succeed. For m=ceil(log2p), the m-bit experiment includes b0 and every b>=p as explicit FAILURE outcomes rather than resampling them for free. Therefore its exact success probability is theta=(p-1)/2^(m+1). The inequality2^m<2p gives theta>(p-1)/(4p)>=1/6>1/8 for p>=3. Completely split n>=2 in fact forces p>=5, but the weaker proof is sufficient.

Condition on the fixed basis, p and ANY complete previous trial transcript, including all failed b values. Fresh m independent fair bits give the same theta again. Thus P(all L trials fail)=(1-theta)^L. This product is justified by literal conditional independence, not marginal probabilities after selection. Every draw has exactly m bits and the trial budget is finite; there is no uncapped field-uniform rejection loop. Stopping on the first success can affect which root is chosen, but every chosen root has exact order2n, so no distributional uniformity of that root is needed downstream.

For each successful alpha, the n odd powers alpha^j,1<=j<2n, are exactly all primitive2n-th roots. Because n is a power of two, these are also all roots of X^n+1. A different first alpha merely permutes this set. RS interval recognition and GAP maximization search that entire set, so they cannot miss a favorable ordering because of the root sampling. A cyclic support shift is retained. Basis evaluations require only n roots and n columns, followed by polynomial-size modular linear algebra and polynomially many interval/gap checks. The source support is recovered from the ideal's root-vanishing subspace; this is not a factorization oracle.
