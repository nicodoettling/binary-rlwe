# SV-LIST-DECODE-v1 author proof

Write a=p*lambda in R. The inverse-ideal/CRT identity and Fourier inversion from SV-RS-DECODE-v1 express its residue coefficients as a_i=c_i*f(x_i), where x_i=alpha^(-2i) are distinct, c_i=alpha^(-i) are nonzero, and deg(f)<t. Cyclic shifts only change c_i. Let z_i be exact nearest-integer rounding of pT_i. Form the list L_i={c_i^(-1)*(z_i+v) modp : -B<=v<=B}, of size2B+1. If f(x_i) is absent, then |z_i-a_i|>=B+1, and hence |p*w_i|>=B+1/2. Parseval gives ||w||_2<=r, so at most h=floor(p^2r^2/(B+1/2)^2) lists miss their true symbol.

Take all monomials X^i Y^j with i+(t-1)j<=d. There are exactly N(d,t) of them. The n(2B+1) homogeneous evaluation constraints Q(x_i,y)=0 for y in L_i therefore have a nonzero solution over F_p. Any such Q has deg_X Q(X,f(X))<=d. It vanishes at the at least n-h distinct good evaluation points; since d<n-h, Q(X,f(X)) is identically zero. The true f is among the polynomial roots of Q.

Here is a deterministic polynomial root-enumeration procedure with numeric-p complexity. Divide Q by the largest common power of X in its coefficients, so Q(0,Y) is nonzero. Enumerate its roots c in F_p by scanning p elements. For each root substitute Y=c+X*Y, again divide by the largest common power of X, and recurse, recording the next coefficient of f. After t levels, substitute the recorded polynomial into the ORIGINAL Q and retain it only if the polynomial identity is zero. Every root f of degree<t survives this process.

The branching bound is essential. At any node, let c have multiplicity m in the nonzero polynomial Q_node(0,Y). In the expansion Q_node(X,c+XY), the term of X-degree m from its X=0 part is nonzero, so the least X-degree s among all terms is at most m. After dividing by X^s, the new polynomial at X=0 has Y-degree at most s<=m. Thus the total degrees at X=0 of all child nodes are at most the parent's degree at X=0. At each recursion depth there are at most deg_Y(original Q) nonempty children, with this degree as a conserved upper budget. Nodes with nonzero constant evaluation polynomial have no children. The tree has O(t*deg_Y Q) nodes. Substitution increases X-degree by at most deg_Y Q per level; all degrees and coefficient arrays stay polynomial. Scanning all p field elements at each node is polynomial in the numeric p. This avoids assuming a separate bivariate factoring theorem.

For each retained f, recover the residue vector a modp and lift coefficientwise to the point lambda_f closest to T among vectors with those residues divided by p. The true lift is obtained because r<1/2. Test membership in J^-1 exactly by checking integrality of lambda_f times every supplied ideal basis element, and test ||T-lambda_f||_2^2<=r^2 by rational arithmetic. The true candidate passes. At most one candidate passes: if0!=u in J^-1, then uJ is a nonzero integral ideal, hence |Norm(u)|*D_J is a positive integer. AM-GM over all embeddings and coefficient Parseval imply ||u||_2>=|Norm(u)|^(1/n)>=D_J^(-1/n). Two passing candidates would differ by a vector of norm at most2r, contradicting (2r)^n D_J<1. This proves deterministic recovery under the promise.

For the explicit regime, n is even and d=n/2. For t>=2, counting by a decreasing left Riemann sum gives N(d,t)>=d^2/(2(t-1))>=n^2/(8t). Since n>=64t, B=floor(n/(64t)) satisfies B>=n/(128t)>=1/2 and B>=1; moreover2B+1<=3n/(64t), so n(2B+1)<N(d,t). The split-prime condition gives p>=2n+1>2B+1. The radius bound yields p*r<=B*sqrt(n)/2, hence h<=n/4. Consequently d=n/2<n-h. If D_J<=2^(rho n) and r<1/(4sqrt(n)), then2r<1/(2sqrt(n))<2^(-rho) for n>=2 and rho<1, so (2r)^n D_J<1.
