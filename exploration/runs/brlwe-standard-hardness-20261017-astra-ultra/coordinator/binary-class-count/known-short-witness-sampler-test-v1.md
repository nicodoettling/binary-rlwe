# Known short witness: the wide sampler is already available

PROVISIONAL primary-source application and explicit-normalizer check. This tests the same fractional-family bridge after the fixed-linear extraction obstruction. It is not a public attack when the witness is private, and it does not assert that finding the witness is easy.

Let J be any nonzero fractional ideal of R=Z[zeta_(2n)], with a full rational coefficient basis supplied. Suppose the reduction ALSO knows an explicit nonzero f in J. Its n rotations f,Xf,...,X^(n-1)f are linearly independent over Q, belong to J, and all have coefficient norm ||f||. They span the principal sublattice fR. Thus they are an executable full-rank short set, not merely an existential basis bound. In the padded construction J=IH/c, cR subset I, any supplied f in H is such a witness because H subset J.

The primary GPV draft dated November18,2007, [Section3.3, Lemma3.5](https://www.mit.edu/~vinodv/papers/trapcvp.pdf), gives Gaussian sampling from an arbitrary lattice using a known short full-rank subset. It samples a uniform quotient representative modulo the subset lattice and then a centered coset Gaussian. Its stated width exceeds the subset's longest vector by an omega(sqrt(log n)) factor; its runtime is polynomial in input representation. Section3.1 also describes truncating the scalar rejection loop. This is the exact dated version read, not a claim that a later theorem has identical numbering or constants. The PDF was directly readable; two other author URLs failed ordinary retrieval. No hardness theorem is imported from it.

Applying that interface to fR subset J, rational denominators can be cleared in BOTH the ambient and sublattice basis and in the Gaussian width; the dimension and width-to-basis ratio do not change. At W>=||f|| omega(sqrt(log n)), there is a classical polynomial-time sampler negligibly close to D_(J,W), using the public basis and the KNOWN f. The quotient J/fR may be exponentially large, but uniform sampling uses its Smith/Hermite representation and time polynomial in its bit length, not enumeration of every coset. This application must retain the input length of the rational basis, f and W. Explicit bounded finite constants below remain a separate implementation obligation; a primary asymptotic assertion is not a certified bounded-bit wrapper by itself.

For the hypothetical fractional chain W>=32sqrt2 n^2 and a supplied nonzero ternary f, ||f||<=sqrt(n); hence W/||f|| grows much faster than sqrt(log n). Its Gaussian-sampling output interface is already efficiently simulable with that witness. Any subsequent polynomial-time classical postprocessing using only polynomially many ordinary samples can replace those calls with the simulator, losing at most the sum of the per-call statistical errors, conditioning on past public choices. A quantum algorithm that receives ordinary classical samples can likewise use those samples; a coherent sampling-state/oracle interface is a DIFFERENT requirement and is not supplied by classical distributional closeness.

This does not prove that the source D_(J^-1,s) mod q is easy, that a GP distinguisher has no other useful behavior, or that every nonlinear extraction fails. It says precisely that a worst-case reduction which constructs such a known-witness J and then uses the distinguisher solely to obtain this broad ordinary DGS interface gains no unavailable sampling operation at that stage. If J has a short witness only by a counting argument, the simulator is NOT executable without finding it. Likewise, the proof-only Gaussian in the accepted A interface never supplies the reduction with a hidden witness for free.

An explicit check of the entire sampler law avoids the false assertion that a uniform coset followed by an arbitrary Gaussian marginal is exactly D_(J,W). Let B be the rotation basis of fR, let b_i^* be its Gram-Schmidt vectors, and put s_i=W/||b_i^*||. Exact randomized nearest-plane recursion samples each integer coefficient from its correctly shifted one-dimensional Gaussian, working backwards. For a resulting x in a chosen coset u+fR, orthogonality makes the product of the unnormalized factors exactly exp(-pi||x||^2/W^2). The selected centers depend on previously sampled coefficients and must be retained.

Write M=|J/fR| and C=M product_i s_i. If the scalar Poisson bounds uniformly in every shift give

    rho_(s_i)(Z+center)/s_i in [1-epsilon_0,1+epsilon_0],

then the ACTUAL joint output mass P(x) lies between

    exp(-pi||x||^2/W^2)/(C(1+epsilon_0)^n)
    and exp(-pi||x||^2/W^2)/(C(1-epsilon_0)^n).

Summing this inequality over every x in J, using sum P=1, also bounds the target normalizer rho_W(J)/C. Therefore P(x)/D_(J,W)(x) lies between ((1-epsilon_0)/(1+epsilon_0))^n and its reciprocal. Both normalizers are accounted for; there is no independence claim about the selected scalar centers. For example epsilon_0=epsilon/(8n),0<epsilon<=1/2 yields TV<=epsilon: log of the reciprocal is at most3n epsilon_0<=3epsilon/8, and exp(3epsilon/8)-1<epsilon. Scalar approximations and finite uniform-quotient cutoffs require their own additional TV allocations.

A sufficient elementary scalar width is min_i s_i>=sqrt(log(32n/epsilon)/pi)+1: Poisson gives an error at most2 sum_(j>=1)exp(-pi s_i^2 j^2), bounded by epsilon/(8n) under these conservative constants. Since ||b_i^*||<=||f||, it is enough to impose this on W/||f||. This explicit inequality is a coordinator derivation; it is not quoted as a constant from GPV. Complete finite scalar implementation and exact review are still open for this new note, so it is not added to the accepted theorem list.

Exact finite normalizer countercontrol: take five support points {-1,-1/2,0,1/2,1} with weights2^(-4x^2), partitioned into integer and half-integer cosets. Their masses are9/8 and1. Uniform selection of the two cosets followed by their normalized conditional weights gives coset masses1/2,1/2. The globally normalized target gives9/17,8/17. Their total variation is1/34, not zero. This finite conditional model is not the full Gaussian and does not certify a smoothing bound; it demonstrates why the two coset normalizers cannot be discarded. All arithmetic is exact and no computation was launched for this hand control.

The next discriminating input is therefore whether a proposed family construction supplies a short witness to the reduction, merely proves one exists, or can reach a narrow Gaussian regime where the public full-rank set no longer enables this sampler. Any such change needs both planted/comparator endpoints and the original binary-law composition checked anew.
