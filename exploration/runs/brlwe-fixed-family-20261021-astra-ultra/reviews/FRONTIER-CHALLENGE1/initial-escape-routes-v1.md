## FF escapes to reconcile

F1. **Exact or negligible-error joint channel.** A randomised correction/rejection kernel might preserve both planted and comparator laws while obtaining the exact target discrete Gaussian conditional on the entire public bank and one secret. Require uniform negligible error independent of fixed polynomial sample exponent, or exact simulation. A scalar variance calculation is insufficient. Next test: explicit channel, then finite n=2/moduli up to 13 LP or exact enumeration of joint law; 1–3 research hours, 1 minute/512 MiB controls.

F2. **Computational rather than total-variation transfer.** The bad output-law distance may be invisible to the particular supplied code even if a universally quantified statistical map fails. A code-dependent bias correction or estimator might use controlled oracle interpolation without changing the fixed target. Need an exact usable inequality bounding the distinguisher gap and its all-input evaluation cost. First test: reduce arbitrary Boolean code to its acceptance function and identify which functional is estimated; 2–4 hours, no heavy experiment initially.

F3. **Direct fixed-modulus fibre/information theorem.** Avoid auxiliary-modulus transfer by proving a selected-functional theorem at one fixed q, perhaps exploiting nonuniform multiplicities, entropy rather than max fibres, or exceptional-character stratification. A bad one-dimensional selected direction need not control all aggregate directions used by a reduction. First test: formulate fixed-q statistic and find a finite adversarial secret partition at n=2,4; 2 hours plus <=120-second exact control.

F4. **A different encoding of the useful information.** Rather than exposing the complete fibre selector, use a random linear sketch, a hash, or a soft likelihood statistic. Low mutual information or average-case conditional min-entropy may suffice where worst-case fibre size fails. The public information available to the algorithm must be distinguished from a latent proof variable. First test: exact conditional law and required information inequality; 2–3 hours.

F5. **Secret randomisation beyond a single affine symmetry orbit.** Known affine transformations preserving binary secrets have limited orbits; a stochastic map, an embedding followed by a valid recovery, or an interactive reset could in principle mix across them. Need exact uniform binary-secret law and independent target errors, not merely secret-counting symmetry. First test: classify all target-law-preserving stochastic maps at n=2 before assuming a general impossibility; 2–4 hours, small finite LP if defined.

F6. **Use good-secret mass without pointwise amplification.** A decision distinguisher with average advantage may yield a productive random-secret trial with probability polynomial in epsilon; a reduction may locate or exploit this using known-secret calibration and restart, rather than amplifying on one unknown secret. Must couple calibration and hidden-centre use through one provable invariant. First test: adversarial rare-good-secret example and exact success accounting; 1–2 hours.

F7. **Dimension or sample compression with the target family unchanged.** A source dimension transformation, block embedding, or restriction may leave the queried target n on the one fixed family while auxiliary resources grow. Conversely, choosing the target degree as a function of adversarial exponents may violate the objective. First test: a complete quantifier diagram naming which n indexes the source and target; 1 hour before any construction.

F8. **Code access and nonblack-box reductions.** The permitted full code and all-input time bound could permit a structural or circuit-based argument unavailable to black-box channels. A universal exhaustive code analysis is not polynomial; a restricted code class could still isolate a useful boundary. First test: explicitly list how code is used and charge circuit size/time; 1–2 hours.

F9. **Secret-dependent direction plus a global estimator.** Failure of a fixed direction does not preclude averaging learned directions, a list of candidates, or a multi-direction decoder that tolerates some bad subproblems. Need a true joint law and measurable selection rule. First test: formulate rank/coverage condition over all nonzero directions and identify an ideal giving a global obstruction; 2–4 hours.

## Source-significance escapes to reconcile

S1. **Acquire a short certificate from an ordinary basis.** Algorithms for fixed-support ideal elements, structured principal generators, HNF/SNF relations, subfields, or spectral gaps may efficiently produce a usable multiplier. Existence is not acquisition. First test: fixed-support integer lattice construction and exact bit cost, then compare acquired norm times actual r with 1/2; 1–3 hours.

S2. **Large quotient exponent need not mean hard decoding.** Ideals can have exponential quotient exponent but a short known or recoverable noninteger multiplier. Exhibit such a family with exact determinant/index and guard margins, preferably acquisition from ordinary input alone. First test: a quadratic-subfield principal family, exact recurrence for norms and coefficients, and ordinary-basis recovery; 1–2 hours.

S3. **List decoding and verification.** If coordinate rounding fails, enumerate a polynomial list in a sparse/structured quotient and verify ideal membership and actual error norm. First test: show the list bound at r delivered by the reduction, including q in bit complexity; 1–3 hours.

S4. **Transfer from an independently hard family with full algorithms.** A fractional ideal map, subfield tower, direct sum, tensor construction, or reduction between BDD and DGS could preserve a known hard source while meeting the norm bound. Must prove maps in both needed directions and exact distortion, not infer hardness from a missing decoder. First test: explicit source/target endpoints and actual r; 3–6 hours, likely unresolved.

S5. **Residual-regime complexity classification.** If existing criteria cover only some ideals, characterize inputs escaping them in computable invariants and compare to established algorithms at the actual radius. A substantial new easy family can complete the diagnostic obligation without proving hardness or solving FF. First test: produce a family simultaneously outside all accepted sufficient tests, then identify whether that is merely a test failure or an actual hardness hypothesis; 2–4 hours.
