# Proof of SV-FROB-PRIME v1

Frozen author proof, PROVISIONAL.

For odd p, sigma_p is a Galois automorphism because gcd(p,2n)=1. Its order is f_res=ord_(2n)(p), and the fixed field has degree g=n/f_res over Q. The residue degree of every prime above p is f_res, so D=p^f_res. The exact cyclotomic residue-degree input is checked in primary-source-check-v1.md (Milne Example8.18 or Weston PropositionIII.3.3). The fundamental Galois fixed-field degree formula is used with the cyclic subgroup generated by sigma_p.

On a coefficient basis vector X^i, sigma_p gives X^(pi), reduced modulo X^n+1. Its coordinate permutation is i→pi modn and its sign is (-1)^floor(pi/n). Since p is odd this is a permutation. Following one permutation cycle expresses every fixed coefficient as plus/minus its initial coefficient. If the product of signs is−1, invariance requires c=−c, hence c=0 over the integers; that cycle contributes nothing. If the product is+1, arbitrary integer initial coefficient produces exactly one invariant signed orbit vector. Distinct cycles have disjoint support. This proves the stated vectors form an integral basis of the fixed coefficient lattice V=R intersect Fix(sigma_p). There are g of them because its Q-span is the fixed field. Every coefficient of a binary combination is0 or the fixed orbit sign; differences of binary combinations are ternary, with norm at mostsqrt(n).

Let reduction modulo J take V into R/J=F_(p^f_res). For x=Σa_iX^i in V, one has sigma_p(x)=x exactly. In characteristic p,

    x modJ = sigma_p(x) modJ = (x modJ)^p,

because a_i^p=a_i modp. The fixed elements of pth powering in a finite field are exactly its prime subfield F_p: they are roots of Y^p−Y, which has p distinct roots already supplied by the constants. Thus the image of V has at mostp elements and, since constants lie in V, exactlyp elements. This argument neither assumes uniform residues nor requires an explicit finite-field coordinate system.

Eligibility gives p^f_res<=2^(rho n), hence p<=2^(rho g)<2^g. Since p is an integer, p+1<=2^g and h=ceil(log2(p+1))<=g. Enumerate any p+1 distinct binary combinations of the first h invariant basis vectors. There are onlyp residue classes in the image, so two sums collide. They are distinct because the b_i are independent, and their difference f is nonzero, ternary, and inJ, with norm<=sqrt(n).

Computing the signed permutation, its cycles, and its basis costs polynomial n and logp arithmetic. The residue key A^-1x modulo Z^n is exact and polynomial in full input bits. Computing and storing p+1 keys costs polynomial numeric p and input bits. No enumeration overp^f_res and no ordinary-basis reduction algorithm yielding a short vector is assumed.
