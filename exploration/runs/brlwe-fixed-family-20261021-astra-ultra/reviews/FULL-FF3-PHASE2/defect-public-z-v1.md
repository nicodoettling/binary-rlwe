The exact public extension compressed-selector-public-extension-v1.md does not restrict Z to a discrete alphabet, but imposes its conditional normalization and bounds only for every positive-mass z. This is insufficient for nonatomic public information. The core information theorem's every-x normalization is unaffected.

Counterexample to the literal unrestricted extension: S is uniform on four secrets; X=S; Z is independent Uniform[0,1]. Propose E uniformly from the four secrets. Set C=1[E=X], L=2, G(e,c)=c, M=1, and retain accepted E as output. Proposals and all coin flips are fresh. Rejection sampling is well-defined and succeeds eventually with probability1; its accepted E equals S. Thus P_guess=1>ML/N=1/2 and the expected trial count is4 rather than1. At every z the normalization sum is1/4, but there is no positive-mass z, so the extension's stated conditional premise is vacuous.

The finite public-multiplier application has no such issue. A repair can restrict Z to finite/countable support, or explicitly state regular conditional kernels and enforce normalization and bounds for P_Z-almost every z. The latter needs the relevant measurable-kernel conventions. Until a precise repair is frozen and freshly checked, the unrestricted v1 extension is REJECTED_LITERAL_SCOPE; its finite/discrete special case remains supported. Downstream claims relying on continuous/general Z must reopen; the original core theorem and its Gaussian target corollary do not rely on this defective wording.
