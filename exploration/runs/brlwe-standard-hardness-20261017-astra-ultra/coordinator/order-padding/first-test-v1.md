# Scalar-conductor order and dimension-padding endpoint test v1

PROVISIONAL investigation, not a theorem or standard-hardness import. Recorded2026-10-17. This revisits one concrete gap in the earlier order-colon route: binary bits in the basis of Z+cR are transportable to coefficient bits using two guesses. The source/error/ideal costs still have to work; the basis issue alone is not a complete obstruction.

## 1. Exact secret-basis transport

Let R=Z[X]/(X^N+1), O_c=Z+cR, gcd(c,q)=1. Its standard basis is1,cX,...,cX^(N-1). For independent bits S_i define S_O=S_0+c sum_(i>=1)S_iX^i=cS_R-(c-1)S_0. Uniform a in O_c/qO_c maps to uniform a in R/qR under natural reduction; multiplication by c is a bijection there. Given responses b_i=a_iS_O+E_i, output A_i=c a_i and, for each guessed t in{0,1}, B_(i,t)=b_i+(c-1)a_i t. The correct branch t=S_0 has EXACT law B_i=A_iS_R+E_i. For uniform response comparator, each branch separately remains uniform conditional on A, although the two branches are correlated. No joint independence across the guesses is claimed.

For a recovery oracle, run both guesses with fresh oracle coins. The correct branch's average success is the original oracle's average success on uniform S_R; it is incorrect to assume separate success guarantees conditioned on S_0=0 or1. The correct branch is an analysis choice, not a known executable choice. A candidate list of size2 or fresh validation is needed. This identity alone does not convert uniform Order-LWE secrets into binary O_c secrets. It also does not convert the error convention: coefficient errors, O_c-basis errors, canonical continuous errors and rounded dual-order errors are different full laws.

The dual basis, up to the harmless fixed trace/unit normalization, is1,X/c,...,X^(N-1)/c. Its binary secret has cS_dual=S_R+(c-1)S_0. Multiplying a dual response by c and subtracting the guessed constant contribution gives the same two-branch coefficient-secret identity. The modulus lattice and rounding kernel must be transported explicitly. Physical error widths scale by c; support restrictions do not disappear merely by comparing covariances.

## 2. Why arbitrary lattice padding does not immediately provide a source

Take an arbitrary integral coefficient lattice L with cR contained in L contained in R. Then O_c L is contained in L: this is an elementary stabilization trick, with no claim that L is an R-ideal. Write L^dagger={a: constant_coefficient(a x) is integral for every x in L}. Because cR is contained in L, L^dagger is contained in c^-1 R. It is the coefficient dual with conjugation, not an R-module inverse.

For a in L^dagger and x in L, all coefficients of ax lie in c^-1 Z, and its constant coefficient is integral. Thus ax lies in O_c^dagger. This can support a scalar-order pairing, but it is NOT the coefficientwise integrality ax in R used by the accepted fractional-source theorem. Using the latter without checking every coefficient would import a false endpoint.

If the secret is binary in the dual order basis, its small-coordinate shifts have scale1/c. To mask such shifts one might choose proof-only G in L/c. The required exact product condition aG in O_c^dagger becomes aL contained in cO_c^dagger. The constant-coefficient condition forces a in cL^dagger. Conversely cL^dagger is integral, so its products with L are integral and have constant coefficient divisible by c; it supplies that condition. Therefore the natural candidate multiplier lattice is EXACTLY cL^dagger, an integral lattice, rather than a hidden-denominator over-lattice.

Under natural reduction moduloq, these integral Gaussian lifts remain ordinary short coefficient representatives. If their coefficient width is s and q is much larger than s times a Gaussian tail factor, the planted source is efficiently distinguished by coefficient size, while the comparator is exactly uniform. Thus the denominator hiding needed by the prior fractional-source construction has disappeared. This is an explicit endpoint diagnosis, not evidence that the resulting source is hard.

For the primal order basis, masking its c-scaled bit shifts with cL leads to the same cancellation of the denominator after the public multiplier is rescaled by c in Section1. This is the precise cost that the easy two-guess basis transport does not remove.

## 3. A concrete padding control

Let L=pZ^m direct-sum Z^(N-m), 1<=m<N, with the unconstrained coefficient positions including any monomial. Taking c=p makes it an O_p module. As an R-module, however, the R-ideal generated by L is ALL of R, since an unconstrained monomial is a unit. Therefore the R-module inverse (R:L) is just R. An attempted R-ideal source based on that colon forgets every hard coordinate constraint. The coefficient dual retains the constraints but is not stable under multiplication by X; multiplying one constrained fractional basis vector into an unconstrained position is an explicit failure.

Adding many unconstrained coordinates can make the determinant per ambient dimension small and can create abundant binary ambiguity. It also introduces known short directions and, in the candidate order-pairing map above, an integral short-source distinguisher. Neither low determinant after padding nor the two-guess secret-basis identity proves a standard arbitrary-lattice reduction.

Next possible discriminator: a different order pairing/source law would need both an executable hidden-denominator endpoint after the binary-secret transport and an explicit downstream short-vector guarantee for the original, unpadded lattice. No such map is supplied here. All claims are local to these displayed candidates.
