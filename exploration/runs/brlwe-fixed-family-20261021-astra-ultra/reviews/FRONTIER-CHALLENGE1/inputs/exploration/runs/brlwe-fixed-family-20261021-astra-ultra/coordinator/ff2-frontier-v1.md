# FF2 initial alternatives

These are investigation records, not accepted claims.

1. Fixed-q selected-value success has an intrinsic 1/q baseline: a predictor can guess the most frequent value at any fixed root. Since epsilon may be n^-c for arbitrarily large fixed c while q has fixed polynomial exponent, the inherited absolute success-versus-guessing argument cannot merely reuse a 1/q-scale upper bound. This only obstructs that comparison, not a fixed-family decision reduction.

2. The inherited norm-divisor result averages DISTINCT primes over a COMMON binary partition. Repeating q adds no new norm divisors; changing the partition with q breaks its pair-count interchange. No replacement proof has been supplied here.

3. Candidate symmetry route: for odd t modulo2n, phi_t(f)=f(X^t) is a signed coefficient permutation. Adding the known vector c_t which is1 on negative-sign output positions sends phi_t(S)+c_t bijectively to the binary cube. Transform (A,B) to (phi_t(A), phi_t(B)+phi_t(A)c_t). This preserves the exact shared binary planted law, independent coefficient symmetric Gaussian errors and independent uniform public multipliers; comparator responses remain uniform. Investigate whether this can turn one selected value into sufficiently rich information. Good-secret concentration and selection after symmetry may defeat a naive repeated-extraction proof; fresh decision coins alone do not resolve them.

4. Alternative statistic: selected-value EXCESS over its source posterior baseline, rather than raw correctness. Need a bounded executable test and a lower bound from decision advantage with both normalizers and source support controlled. Subtracting a computable uniform1/q baseline is unjustified when source posterior bins are nonuniform. Computing the exact posterior may itself be hard.

5. Noise projection route: with one fixed sufficiently large coefficient Gaussian, some low-dimensional CRT projections may be exponentially close to uniform. If proved jointly and uniformly for the projections actually revealed, this could rule out low-information decision witnesses and require extraction of many coordinates. Scalar CRT marginals do not imply joint uniformity. Exact arithmetic/Fourier lattice bounds and whole-batch costs are required.
