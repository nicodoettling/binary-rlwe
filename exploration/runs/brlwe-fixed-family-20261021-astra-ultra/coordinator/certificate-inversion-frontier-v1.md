# Identifying certificates and the unproved efficient inverse

Research note, not a new accepted theorem. CRT-PROJ-v1 and CERT-EXTRACT-v2 identify S from r root values with q^(2r)>n^n. For the selected root set T, let I_T be the integral ideal of polynomials vanishing on T modulo q. Its index is q^r. Interpolating the reported values supplies a residue class z+I_T containing S. Every nonzero f in I_T has coefficient norm at least q^(r/n)>sqrt(n), by the same norm-divisor proof. The cube centre u=(1/2,...,1/2) has distance exactly sqrt(n)/2 from every binary S. Thus the certificate inversion problem is a promised lattice closest-vector problem for the coset z+I_T, with centre u and at most one vector at that distance.

This reformulation does not yield a polynomial algorithm: standard nearest-plane guarantees depend on supplied basis quality, and the known lower bound alone supplies no short basis. The ideal index q^r is at least n^(n/2), outside the inherited source norm cap2^(rho n). No unproved efficient embedding back into that source family is made.

Attempt to recover all but O(log n) roots then enumerate residual binary coordinates: arbitrary root-evaluation matrices in power-of-two dimension are NOT automatically MDS. Consecutive root rows do give a Vandermonde support bound, but CERT's roots are adaptively selected by an arbitrary distinguisher. Restricting selection to a desired set can destroy the gap: the distinguisher may use only excluded coordinates. This alternative needs a new controlled-selection theorem or a norm/theta estimate for every adaptively remaining set; the current result supplies neither. Increasing the noise to make the whole response negligible would destroy a useful decision premise, not solve this gap.
