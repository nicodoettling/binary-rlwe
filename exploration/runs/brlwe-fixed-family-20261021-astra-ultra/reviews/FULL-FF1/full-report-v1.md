## Claim graph and arithmetic audit

CRT-DUAL-v1 depends on the split-prime CRT isomorphism, integer multiplication matrices and Smith normal form. For nonzero u, z_i = sum u_alpha alpha^i mod q. Evaluating Z at beta^-1 gives n u_beta for beta in T and zero otherwise. Both alpha and beta are roots of X^n+1, so alpha/beta is an nth root of unity. Inversion preserves the root set because n is even. Since q does not divide n, Z is nonzero mod q and therefore nonzero in Q[X]/(X^n+1). The rational polynomial X^n+1 is irreducible for the required power-of-two n. Thus its norm is a nonzero integer.

There are at least n-h zero CRT evaluations of Z. The multiplication matrix has rank at most h mod q, so at least n-h integer Smith entries are divisible by q; q^(n-h) divides the determinant. This counts distinct CRT coordinates at one prime and is not an illicit repetition of a prime in the historical averaging lemma.

Let omega_j be the complex roots. Parseval gives sum_j |Z(omega_j)|^2 = n sum_i z_i^2. AM-GM therefore gives |N(Z)| <= ||z||_2^n, with no extra sqrt(n) in the coefficient norm. Combined with divisibility this gives ||z||_2 >= q^(1-h/n). Centering yields the exact squared distance sum >= q^(-2h/n). Zero coefficients and zero CRT values remain allowed; only the whole character is nonzero.

CRT-FOURIER-v1 uses the normalized exact integer Gaussian with weights exp(-pi x^2/sigma^2). Poisson gives phi(theta) = sigma/rho_sigma(Z) times the positive dual Gaussian sum. The normalization identity rho_sigma(Z)=sigma rho_(1/sigma)(Z)>=sigma is retained. For 0<=theta<=1/2 the m=0 and m=1 relative terms total at most 2. For m=-j, j>=1, the squared-distance increment is j^2+2j theta>=j^2; for m=j+1 it is at least j(j+1)>=j^2. With sigma>=1 the remaining relative terms are bounded by 2 sum_j>=1 exp(-pi j^2)<1. Symmetry handles negative theta. Hence the factor 3 is valid including theta=1/2 and sigma=1. Independence is invoked only for the original coefficient errors, giving 3^n. Finite-group Parseval and Cauchy-Schwarz sum every nonzero character and give TV <= (1/2)sqrt(sum |phi_u|^2), hence the displayed q^(h/2) factor. A single-character argument would not suffice.
