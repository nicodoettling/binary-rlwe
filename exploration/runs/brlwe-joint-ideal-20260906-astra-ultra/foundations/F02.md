## F02-N1: a uniform-in-width wrapped obstruction

Fix odd q, deterministic F in (Z/qZ)[X]/(X^n+1), tau >= tau0 > 0, G with iid D_tau coefficients, and H independent of the entire G vector. Suppose TV(FG+H mod q, W_sigma,q^n) <= epsilon. For r_k=center_q(f_k),

    max_k r_k^2 <= (pi sigma^2 + 2 q^2 epsilon)/(16 D_tau0(1)).

Proof. Set r=exp(-pi/tau^2). The theta product (NIST DLMF 20.5.3, https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.5.E3) gives

    phi_tau(theta) = product_{m>=1} [1 - a(r^(2m-1)) sin^2(pi theta)],
    a(v)=4v/(1+v)^2.

Every factor lies in (0,1] because 0<v<1, and a'(v)=4(1-v)/(1+v)^3 >= 0. Every finite product decreases as tau increases. Passing to the convergent positive product shows phi_tau(theta) <= phi_tau0(theta), for every real theta and every tau>=tau0.

The zero coefficient of FG is f_0 G_0 - sum_{k>=1} f_k G_{n-k}. Independence and positivity of the Gaussian characteristics therefore imply

    phi_sigma(1/q)-2epsilon <= |E exp(2pi i(FG+H)_0/q)|
                             <= product_k phi_tau(f_k/q)
                             <= phi_tau(f_j/q) <= phi_tau0(f_j/q).

The prior elementary inequalities are 1-phi_tau0(r_j/q) >= 16 D_tau0(1) r_j^2/q^2 and 1-phi_sigma(1/q) <= pi sigma^2/q^2. The latter uses the exact discrete-Gaussian variance upper bound Var(D_sigma)<=sigma^2/(2pi), rather than a continuous approximation. Combining proves the displayed bound.

A useful strengthening uses the whole coefficient product. If d=pi sigma^2/q^2+2epsilon<1, then

    sum_j r_j^2 <= -q^2 log(1-d)/(16 D_tau0(1)).

Indeed phi_tau0(r_j/q)<=1-16D_tau0(1)r_j^2/q^2<=exp(-16D_tau0(1)r_j^2/q^2), and the product is at least 1-d. For d<=1/2 this implies sum_j r_j^2<=(pi sigma^2+2q^2epsilon)/(8D_tau0(1)). This is an additional necessary coefficient-norm condition, not the continuous operator condition and not a construction.

For random source variables C=(J,U,F), their identical marginals in the joint noise comparison imply E_C epsilon(C)<=eta_noise. Markov's inequality at any beta>0 yields the coefficient bound with epsilon=eta_noise/beta outside a C-event of mass beta. If q is polynomial and eta_noise negligible, take beta=sqrt(eta_noise); outside negligible probability every centered F coefficient has magnitude O_tau0(sigma)+negl(n). This remains true even if tau grows arbitrarily fast. The exact statement still requires the iid Gaussian law to survive the conditioning on C.

Consequently the prior binomial-source block decoder applies at q/sigma>=n^c and L=Theta(log^3 n), with overwhelming planted acceptance and uniform false acceptance at most L q (|E|/q)^L. This conclusion concerns that binomial support family. It is not an attack on arbitrary rich CRT supports or a general impossibility of public-only simulators.

## F02-F1: exact wrapped deconvolution is a positivity problem

The continuous diagnostic is precise within its own experiment. If G is a real centered Gaussian of covariance tau^2 I/(2pi), then an independent real Gaussian H can make MG+H exactly N(0,sigma^2 I/(2pi)) if and only if sigma^2 I-tau^2 M M^T is positive semidefinite. In an exact unwrapped discrete-Gaussian completion, independence instead forces Var(D_tau) M M^T <= Var(D_sigma) I, using actual discrete variances. Neither argument may be applied to arbitrary wrapped laws by treating centered representatives as an ordinary sum: modular carries alter the covariance identity. The finite-group criterion below directly addresses that experiment.

Identify the additive group of R_q with K=(Z/qZ)^n. For fixed F write mu_F=law(M_F G mod q), nu=W_sigma,q^n, and use characters exp(2pi i<t,x>/q). Their transforms are

    muhat_F(t)=product_j phi_tau((M_F^T t)_j/q)>0,
    nuhat(t)=product_j phi_sigma(t_j/q)>0.

Positivity of the Gaussian characteristic follows either from its Poisson expression or the theta product. Consequently the only possible law for an EXACT independent H is the inverse Fourier transform of

    r_F(t)=nuhat(t)/muhat_F(t),
    h_F(x)=q^(-n) sum_{t in K} r_F(t) exp(-2pi i<t,x>/q).         (F1)

It has total mass 1 and is real because the quotient is even. Existence is equivalent to h_F(x)>=0 for every x. Equivalently r_F must be positive definite: for every complex coefficient vector (a_t), sum_{s,t} a_s conjugate(a_t) r_F(s-t)>=0. The equivalence follows by Fourier diagonalizing this finite circulant matrix, whose eigenvalues are |K|h_F(x). The elementary magnitude condition r_F(t)<=1 is necessary but insufficient. Conditioning on J,U,F does not create extra freedom for exact H: for fixed F the exact deconvolution law, if it exists, is unique.

This inverse transform enumerates q^n points and is not by itself a polynomial-time sampler. Even if h_F>=0, approximate evaluation requires quantitative precision and an efficient sampling representation. Also an exponentially small denominator muhat_F(t) makes Fourier division poorly conditioned. Negativity of an exact quotient does not by itself give an asymptotically useful lower bound on best approximate convolution; a separating inequality or explicit error analysis is needed. The smoothing construction in F02-completion.md provides a representation and an approximate sampler without attempting exponential Fourier inversion.

For approximate correctness, a basic necessary inequality at every t is

    eta_noise(F) >= (nuhat(t)-muhat_F(t))_+/2,

since an independent H has characteristic of modulus at most one. This is useful as an obstruction and supplies N1 at low frequencies, but it is only one class of separating tests.

## F02-F2: a hand counterexample using actual wrapped Gaussians

This is a fixed finite diagnostic on Z/5, with F=1 and no secret. Set

    exp(-pi/tau^2)=r=1/100,
    exp(-pi/sigma^2)=s=1/50.

Then sigma>tau, so the unwrapped continuous complement sigma^2-tau^2 is positive. Moreover phi_sigma(theta)<=phi_tau(theta) at every theta by the product proof above. Thus every exact Fourier quotient lies in (0,1]. Nevertheless no independent H can complete wrapped D_tau to wrapped D_sigma.

Write the two wrapped symmetric laws as p=(p0,p1,p2,p2,p1) and t=(t0,t1,t2,t2,t1). Direct bounds on their defining Gaussian sums give

    p0>.98,   .009<p1<.011,   p2<2*10^(-8),
    t1>.019,  t2<2*10^(-7).                                  (F2)

For reproducibility by hand: the scalar normalizer is Z(r)=1+2r+2r^4+2r^9+... . At r<=1/50 it lies between 1+2r and 1+2r+3r^4, since the remaining tail is bounded by a geometric series. The wrapped numerator at residue 1 is r+r^16+r^36+..., and at residue 2 it is r^4+r^9+r^49+...; the same substitution with s gives the target. These inequalities immediately imply the deliberately loose rational bounds F2.

Any successful completion law may be symmetrized by averaging with its reflection, since p and t are symmetric. Thus write H=(x,y,z,z,y), where x,y,z>=0 and x+2y+2z=1. Convolution at residues 1 and 2 would require

    t1=p1 x+(p0+p2)y+(p1+p2)z,
    t2=p2 x+(p1+p2)y+(p0+p1)z.

The second equation gives z<t2/p0<3*10^(-7). The first, using x<=1, p0+p2<=1 and p1+p2<.012, gives

    y>=t1-p1-(p1+p2)z>.019-.011-.012*(3*10^(-7))>.0079.

But then t2>p1 y>.009*.0079>7*10^(-5), contradicting F2. Thus the inverse Fourier transform has a negative entry. This example also excludes arbitrarily accurate approximation at these fixed widths; for instance the same inequalities remain contradictory if each t_i is perturbed by at most 10^(-5), which TV distance below 10^(-5) would imply. This is not an impossibility theorem for growing widths or dimensions.

For an exact rational finite-support control independent of Gaussians, take on Z/5 mu=(3/4)Uniform+(1/4)delta_0=(2/5,3/20,3/20,3/20,3/20), so every nontrivial Fourier coefficient is 1/4. Take nu=(3/10,17/80,11/80,11/80,17/80). Direct convolution gives nu=mu*h for the signed mass h=(3/5,1/4,-1/20,-1/20,1/4). The Fourier quotient is 1 at zero, (10+3sqrt(5))/20 at +/-1, and (10-3sqrt(5))/20 at +/-2, all in (0,1]. Its unique inverse h nevertheless has two negative entries. This exact rational finite-support search checks the tempting relaxation that bounded nonnegative Fourier ratios suffice. No numerical experiment was needed or run.

## F02-R1: likelihood comparison and decision

For full joint target and simulated laws P,Q, with M_2(P||Q)=E_Q[(P/Q)^2]=K, Cauchy–Schwarz gives Q(E)>=P(E)^2/K for a positive event E. If E is planted-secret recovery, the original secret must be retained in both laws. This can transfer inverse-polynomial recovery success when K is polynomial; it is not a signed decision-advantage bound.

For a decision test whose target acceptance is p=r+epsilon and reference-uniform acceptance is r, the same calculation gives only

    acceptance_Q >= (r+epsilon)^2/K,
    signed_advantage_Q >= (r+epsilon)^2/K-r,

which can be zero or negative. An exact two-point control is Q=Uniform=(1/2,1/2), P=(1/2+epsilon,1/2-epsilon). Then M_2(P||Q)=1+4epsilon^2, P has distinguishing advantage epsilon against Q, and the simulated law Q has advantage zero. Even a moment close to 1 at a specified inverse-polynomial scale can erase a decision advantage at the corresponding scale.

The general decision bound obtained from this moment is TV(P,Q)<=sqrt(K-1)/2. Thus a quantitative bridge can subtract this term if it is small compared with the actual distinguisher's advantage; uniform negligible-error transfer requires a negligible term, or a separately justified parameter choice and advantage accounting. C1 supplies negligible TV directly, so no Renyi substitution is needed for the present construction.
