## Strongest objection

The exact source needed for the reduction is unsupported by the checked
independent hardness results. On a_2 being a unit, it is exactly a single
binary-secret, binary-error ring sample (A,C=Ax+y), with an independent uniform
unit adjoined. A compact-knapsack name does not provide a separate hardness
basis for that distribution. The first missing implication is an explicit
reduction from a supported source regime to this exact two-binary-block,
negacyclic, planted-input source, with the required success notion.

Neither checked compact-knapsack theorem supplies that implication. Their
precise task and parameter differences are recorded in
[source-audit.md](C03/source-audit.md). No claim is made that all possible
source reductions are impossible.

The source ledger also needs a definite terminology repair. Micciancio's
inversion result is **any-preimage inversion on planted-distribution inputs**,
not recovery of the original sampled witness. Definition 2.5 checks the
function value, and the proof of Theorem 4.8 accepts any member of its solution
relation. C03/source-check.md's phrase “average planted inversion” should be
replaced with that precise wording because C03 itself defines “planted” as
original-pair recovery. The coordinator has been notified; this review does not
silently edit the author's files.

## Mathematical validity and exact scope

For n a power of two, odd q>1, and sigma>0, the centered map

    A'=2a_2^(-1)a_1,
    B'=2a_2^(-1)t-H+G_sigma

is well defined on the unit branch, preserves the actual binary x, and makes
the residual coordinates independent uniform signs plus independent
D_sigma noise. With tau=pi/sigma^2 and
k_c=1+exp(tau)(cosh(2tau)-1), the reviewed finite success bound is

    source planted-pair success >= rho_q epsilon^2/k_c^n.

The exact identity M_2(Q||P)=cosh(2tau) is in the forward direction. The
necessary reverse bound follows from Q>=exp(-tau)P and the corresponding
chi-square comparison. Products are over independent error coefficients;
wrapping contracts the moment. The proof retains (A',x) in the joint law,
and x_hat=x pointwise recovers the original y from C-Ax_hat. Marginalizing
y therefore does not replace the planted witness.

The exact composite-q unit formula and its lower bound
rho_q>=q^(-log_3 2) are correct. For polynomial q, the condition
n/sigma^4=O(log n) gives polynomial loss; sigma^4=Omega(n) bounds the
likelihood factor by a constant, before the separately accounted unit loss.
Effective Gaussian approximation needs the stated joint total variation
error charge. The original uncentered bound also checks out.

These conclusions follow from the displayed finite calculations, rather than
empirical evidence.
Full derivations and exact hand regressions are in
[mathematical-checks.md](C03/mathematical-checks.md).

## Adversarial boundary checks

* A full-support two-point model refutes using M_2(sim||target) in the
  success-transfer denominator. The candidate's reverse-domination step
  avoids the counterexample.
* At tau=log 2, the displayed centered coefficient factor is 13/4, worse
  than the uncentered factor 5/2. The claimed improvement is correctly
  restricted to growing widths.
* Exposing y restores a conditional single-shift moment exp(2tau) per
  coordinate, so the cancellation relies on the solver seeing its specified
  target input. Internal reconstruction using C is legitimate.
* For n=1,q=3,a_1=a_2=1,t=1, two binary preimages exist. Checking a
  preimage does not identify the original pair. The event-level proof tracks
  that original pair explicitly.
* For n=1,q=5,a_1=1,a_2=2, binary inversion is injective and produces no
  collision. A collision theorem cannot be imported through a generic
  “plant a pair, invert, subtract” argument without further analysis.
* Repeated Gaussian additions keep A' identical. Independent target
  multipliers agree only with probability q^(-n), so the elementary equality
  test already refutes treating those repetitions as independent samples.

The last point is an independent scope limitation: an algorithm that succeeds
with polynomially many independent samples need not succeed with exactly one.
The reviewed arrow concerns one-sample search only. It gives no decision
reduction and does not establish the broader reference hardness claim.
