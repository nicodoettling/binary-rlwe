# Endpoint and Gaussian definitions

For source modulus Q and ring R_Q=(Z/QZ)[X]/(X^n+1), P_s is the law of(A,B=A*s+E), with A uniform in R_Q and E independent of A with the specified full ring noise law nu_Q. The law need not have independent coefficients unless expressly stated. U is uniform on R_Q times R_Q: A,B independent uniform. At target modulus q, T_s is(C,D=C*s+E'), with C uniform in R_q and E' independent with full noise law nu_q; V is uniform on R_q times R_q. Candidate secrets are coefficient-binary vectors interpreted in each ring. A target k-sample batch chooses ONE uniform binary S, then k independent pairs conditional on that S, each with fresh independent public multiplier and full error vector. The comparator has independent uniform responses and the same public and metadata law. For finite stated n2 kernels, nu_Q and nu_q are the explicit coefficient product laws in the supplied statement.

For sigma>0, D_(Z,sigma) assigns z in Z mass exp(-pi*z²/sigma²)/sum_(j in Z)exp(-pi*j²/sigma²). A coefficient Gaussian vector uses independent copies. A fixed centered representative interval for modulus m may be taken as I_m={-floor(m/2),... ,ceil(m/2)-1}; it contains m consecutive integers, one per residue. The finite centered Gaussian is D_(Z,sigma) conditioned on membership in I_m and renormalized, then taken independently across coefficients. The wrapped law reduces the original integer Gaussian modulo m. General ENERGY statements allow the stated arbitrary finite consecutive integer alphabets and their ascending integer lexicographic order; the radial finite table uses residues0,...,Q-1 and zero counts, not that Gaussian interval convention.

The additional TWOEVENT statement supplies its own finite source event, endpoint definitions and exact likelihood hypotheses. It is a permitted conditional dependency, not a claim that its hypotheses hold for every parameter choice.
