# M1 classical simulation criterion, v2

Status PROVISIONAL. This statement delimits an oracle, not a standard-hardness theorem. Ordinary coefficient Euclidean norm and Gaussian mass exp(-pi||x-c||²/s²) are used throughout. All input integers and rationals have ordinary binary encodings. Input basis means a nonsingular n by n integer matrix with lattice generated by its columns. Let n>=2, J subset Z^n be the resulting full lattice, and let the squared Gaussian width s²>0 and requested error 0<eta<=1/4 be rational inputs. Define u>=1 by a supplied rational u² satisfying

    4 exp(-pi u²) <= eta/(32n).

A numerical parameter satisfying this sufficient inequality may be chosen with certified slack; deciding exact equality of transcendental quantities is not required.

(1) Given any rational basis B of J with maximum Gram–Schmidt length at most s/u, there is an all-input bounded classical randomized bit algorithm outputting a point of J whose law has TV<=eta from the ordinary centered D_(J,s). It has bit complexity polynomial in n, the complete encoded basis/s²/eta inputs and log(1/eta), provided u>=1 and the displayed inequality holds. This statement concerns classical samples, not coherent access to an arbitrary quantum operation. Scalar sampling, cap failures, finite precision and full conditional normalizers are included. Each fallback is the lattice point zero. The same result applies to a rational lattice given by a rational basis.

(2) From an ordinary integer basis, compute an upper-triangular column Hermite basis H without changing the ambient Euclidean coordinates. Let h=max_i H_ii. Its Gram–Schmidt lengths are H_ii. If e is the least positive integer with e Z^n subset J (largest Smith invariant), every H_ii divides e, hence h<=e. Thus s>=h u suffices, and s>=e u is a computable weaker sufficient criterion. Index det(J) need not be polynomial. These statements require upper-triangular column ordering; an arbitrary basis with small entries or a lower-triangular basis in the same ordering does not inherit the diagonal Gram–Schmidt claim.

(3) Suppose J is an integral ideal of R=Z[X]/(X^n+1), n power-two, D=[R:J], and h0=ceil(log2(D+1))<=n. Enumerating D+1 distinct binary vectors supported in the first h0 coordinates and reducing them modulo J yields a nonzero ternary f in J with ||f||<=sqrt(h0). From such f and the ordinary ideal basis, one can compute a basis with maximum Gram–Schmidt length <=||f|| in polynomial bit time. Enumeration costs O(D+1) reductions, so this is a polynomial-time algorithm for polynomial numeric D, with no claim of polynomial runtime for arbitrary eligible exponential D. Consequently s>=sqrt(h0)u suffices after that preprocessing. Supplied short f also suffices at its charged acquisition cost, regardless of D.

(4) Apply these criteria only to the exact inherited same-J output width

    W=nq/(sqrt(2)d0)=32sqrt(2)n²k chi q/(delta ell²),
    ell=ceil(log2(2nk)), d0=delta ell²/(64nk chi),
    q>=n sigma, sigma=8n²ceil(sqrt(kn))ell⁴,
    k>=1, chi>=1, 0<delta<=1,
    q is an odd prime and q is congruent to1 modulo2n.

Taking eta=2^(-4n), u=4n satisfies (1). The width W exceeds 4n sqrt(n) for all n>=2. Therefore (3) simulates every polynomial-index eligible ideal call at this error, and (2) simulates every ordinary-basis ideal call with h<=W/(4n), in particular e<=W/(4n). The algorithm runtime depends on encoded W², not on the numerical number of integers in a Gaussian interval.

For a concrete exponential-index eligible family, n>=4 power-two, set

    g=X^(n/2)+X^(n/4)-1, J=(3,g) subset R.

Then N(J)=3^(n/2)<=2^(4n/5), e=3, and h<=3. The family is within rho=4/5, and is simulated at width W whenever the rest of the same-J hypotheses hold. No primality of J is asserted or needed. q satisfying those hypotheses is coprime to3.

(5) If a bounded adaptive classical algorithm makes at most L such requests, and for every complete past history the chosen simulator meets its stated full conditional TV bound eta_j, fresh simulator coins give transcript TV at most sum_j eta_j from the corresponding idealized exact-DGS service, capped at1. Replacing an implemented restricted sampler with per-call distance alpha_j from that same reference instead gives at most sum_j(eta_j+alpha_j), under the analogous conditional/reference promise. An unconditional marginal bound for reused, correlated, postselected or coherent queries is insufficient. The result does not supply an arbitrary-precision sampler beyond (1)'s width/accuracy relation.

No conclusion is made for large-HNF-diagonal ideals without an effectively known suitable short basis. Failure of these criteria is not a hardness lower bound. No arbitrary ideal-family transfer or binary-secret computational foundation is established.
