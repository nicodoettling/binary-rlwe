# Rejected or deferred ideas

The following are discovery findings, not a claim that entire techniques are exhausted. C01–C03 were selected separately and have now been reconciled: two structural diagnostics retained, C03’s independent hardness explanation retired while its transfer lemma is preserved. Each note states the restrictions that keep a negative result from becoming a general impossibility claim.

| Mechanism | Evidence and strongest negative finding | Disposition and what could change it |
|---|---|---|
| Statistical quiet planting to uniform (S01) | **Refuted in the stated comparison.** An existential search over binary secrets and a modular residual box distinguishes planted from uniform with overwhelming statistical advantage in the polynomial small-noise gap. This is not an efficient algorithm. | Retain the exact wrapped posterior and annihilator expansion. A computational reduction or a different intermediate experiment must replace statistical closeness to uniform. |
| Posterior transport of the prior (S02) | **Fully written calculation.** Resampling a source posterior can produce the exact binary target joint law, but efficient posterior sampling already carries source-search difficulty. The newly sampled latent secret need not be the original source witness. | Defer until a public simulator with a joint coupling to the original witness is supplied. Matching the public marginal alone is inadequate. |
| Ring-tower packing (S03) | **Fully written coefficient expansion.** The degree-d extension needs d correlated response components; a standard module oracle does not supply them. Trace provides the reverse arrow. Binary radix packing also creates a multiplicative defect. | A new source oracle or response-completion lemma must be named explicitly. Rank, degree, modulus, secret alphabet and error correlation cannot be traded silently. |
| Arbitrary quotient entropy heuristics (S04) | **Exact counterexample and experiment.** At q=3, a family of half-dimensional quotients misses one of nine coefficient pairs per four input bits; TV tends to one even with output entropy below n. Fixed q is a geometric diagnostic outside the reference growing gap. | Replace entropy-only arguments by a distribution-sensitive bound, as in C01. The counterexample does not refute the selected fully split prime regime. |
| Field kernel dimension as Boolean loss (S05) | **Exact calculation.** A half-dimensional kernel can have no nonzero ternary difference. A special n=8,q=7 conference family has both singleton and double Boolean fibers. | Boolean fibers, with their true 2^{-weight} weighting, must be counted. The conference sequence's asymptotic prime requirements are not supplied by finitely many examples. |
| Public masks near simple algebraic codes (S05/C01) | **Explicit algorithms in restricted families.** Consecutive-CRT masks admit Reed–Solomon decoding in the stated sparse-perturbation regime; binomial-quotient complementary ideals admit blockwise enumeration at polynomial q. | These defeat particular samplers, not all hidden ideals or all perturbation widths. Any replacement must state why the relevant public decoder fails. |
| Independent-matrix universality (S07) | **Fully written moment identities.** Unit binary differences can make pair moments agree exactly, while the first three-point moment has ring-commutativity terms absent in the independent-matrix model. A constant-size discrepancy is shown when q² is at least order n Var(E). | Defer raw-moment comparison. Rare likelihood spikes may dominate; a proposed truncated comparison must control them. This is neither an attack nor a general universality impossibility. |
| Public exact symmetry dynamics (S08) | **Fully written calculation.** Noise-preserving affine ring actions mix within orbits of size at most 2n², not across the binary cube. Public adaptive selection among channel symmetries cannot exactly turn an invariant uniform prior into a binary prior while preserving the same joint channel. | Approximate, nonlinear, sample-consuming and noise-changing kernels remain outside this result. Tiny positive Gaussian tails prevent a robust approximation lower bound from being inferred. |
| Low-rank residual characters (S09) | **Conditional attack criterion.** A Fourier vector v with multiplication rank O(1) and inverse-polynomial wrapped-noise Fourier mass would give a polynomial decision test by enumerating q^rank classes. No such vector in the target regime was found. | The next work must construct or exclude that witness under explicit parameter conditions. Fixed-degree algebraic checks and low-degree failures say nothing about unrestricted cryptographic hardness. |
| Coding-source XOR masking (S10) | **Fully written obstruction.** For a unit multiplier over F2, masking the sparse secret into a fully uniform secret makes the one-sample public output uniform and leaves many equally likely planted witnesses. Odd-q lifting additionally introduces witness-dependent integer carries. | A new recoverable source coupling and a valid modulus/noise conversion are both needed. Independently studied QCSD assumptions in other degree and error regimes are not transferred. |
| Sparse quantum Fourier sampling (S11) | **Fully written restricted simulation.** A state supported on K explicitly listed classical sample pairs has Fourier probabilities at most K/q^{2n}; classical rejection sampling simulates that Fourier measurement with polynomial overhead for polynomial K. Secret-annihilator mass is at most K/q^n. | This retires that direct sparse-state mechanism only. Exponentially supported coherent combinations or other quantum processing lie outside the simulation, and no general quantum lower bound is claimed. |
| Hiding sampler coins to restore entropy (S06) | **Exact conditional-independence identity.** If the final error kernel is fixed given A and the secret prior is independent of sampler coins, revealing those coins does not change posterior guessing after observing A,B. | Computational usefulness of a trapdoor is separate. Secret-dependent precursors can over-observe; C02 develops that distinction. |
| Low-index denominator f=1+X (S12) | **Exact positive geometry and explicit negative algorithm.** Only parity is disclosed by its quotient, but the public scalar 2 clears the denominator and exposes short products. | Exact index accounting can matter, but this sampler is distinguishable. The C02 Lucas denominator further separates small index from small quotient exponent without supplying a hidden sampler. |

Unsupported interesting views are preserved in the linked scout notes rather than promoted to assumptions. The exact two-binary-block knapsack source in C03 remains a tailored nearby RLWE problem unless a distinct source-regime bridge is proved. An efficient algorithm contradicting a proposed regime takes precedence over these finite diagnostics.

Criticism also refuted full occupancy of the Lucas quotient (already at n=4) and the unqualified C01 conditional-independence formulation (coins C=G). The corrected compact-width additive template has a written Fourier obstruction. Details and final next questions are in reconciliation.md.
