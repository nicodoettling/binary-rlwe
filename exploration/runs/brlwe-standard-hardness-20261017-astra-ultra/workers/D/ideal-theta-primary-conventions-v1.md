Definition 3.6 samples one elliptical Gaussian distribution, whose errors are independent conditional on that choice. Each squared width includes the deterministic summand α²f(n)²|σ_i(u)|²/(2||u||∞²), with f(n)=ω(sqrt(log n)); the additional latent squares are nonnegative. Thus the lower bound used by D-THETA holds for every ellipse, not merely on average. Theorem 3.7 assumes u∈(R:Q), α∈(0,1), and α/||u||∞>=2ω(1), and its reduction is quantum. Only that displayed width condition is used in the statistical-uniformity corollary. Theorem 6.3 maps ideal-modulus instances to ideal-secret RLWE without changing their error family; this identifies C's proposed endpoint but is not required for the abstract theta argument.

The power-of-two conventions are derived directly: the derivative of X^n+1 is nX^(n-1), whose monomial factor is a unit, giving R∨=(1/n)R; Fourier orthogonality gives canonical squared norm n times coefficient squared norm. Multiplying the dual response torus by nq gives coefficient torus V/qR, and its Gaussian Fourier character evaluates at canonical σ(z). These are exact scalings, not asymptotic approximations.

Its exact endpoint calculation is compatible with D-THETA. Our corollary concerns this map's endpoint, including the common random ellipse. It does not say the original RLWE samples are statistically uniform, prove that a known ideal is easy to decode, or reverse a worst-case/search reduction.
