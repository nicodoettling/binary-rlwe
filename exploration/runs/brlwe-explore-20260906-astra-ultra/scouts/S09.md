# S09 — Conditional Fourier signal and Boolean elimination

Two falsifiable comparisons survive; neither establishes hardness or a target-family attack. Calculations and source checks are in [S09/calculations.md](S09/calculations.md) and [S09/sources.md](S09/sources.md). No CPU experiment was run.

**1. Annihilator Fourier queries — fully written small calculation.** Retain the target ring, binary coefficient secret, wrapped iid coefficient noise, and independent ring samples. Let M_a denote coefficient multiplication, W the n-coordinate wrapped noise mass, and Ŵ(v)=E_e exp(2πi〈v,e〉/q). The bounded residual query

    h_{t,v}(a,b)=Re exp(2πi〈v,b−M_a t〉/q)

has planted expectation Ŵ(v)·1[M_{s−t}ᵀv=0], for nonzero v; its uniform-output expectation is zero even conditioned on a. Indeed, conditioned on a its complex mean is Ŵ(v)exp(2πi〈M_{s−t}ᵀv,a〉/q); averaging uniform a proves the assertion. This measures planting, not public circulant structure.

For ordinary LWE with n independent rows, every nonzero binary difference makes the output difference uniform, so the analogous expectation vanishes. Ring zero divisors permit additional signal. More generally, fixed-secret likelihood ratios L_s relative to uniform pairs satisfy

    〈L_s−1,L_t−1〉 = Σ_{v≠0: M_{s−t}ᵀv=0}|Ŵ(v)|².

This is the noise-weighted version of the ideal-image phenomenon verified in [Boudgoust–Lapiha, Lemma 11 and §3.1](https://hal.science/lirmm-05396888v2/document).

Restrict q to an odd prime and define r(v)=rank(t↦M_tᵀv). Enumerating its q^{r(v)} quotient classes tests all possible residual phases. An efficiently found v with r(v)=O(1) and η=|Ŵ(v)|≥1/poly(n) yields polynomial decision time, using O(η⁻²[r(v)log q+log(1/ε)]) independent ring samples for error ε. It recovers a secret projection, not necessarily the secret. **Unresolved:** no such v is exhibited. Small quotient rank can coexist with negligible Fourier bias after coefficient noise is projected.

The first cheap diagnostic is to enumerate scalar multiples of dual CRT vectors for n=8,16 and split polynomial-sized primes, recording both quotient rank and exact wrapped-noise bias. These are concrete queries in STAT(τ), defined by [Feldman et al., Definition 2.2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2012/064/revision/2/download/). A lower-bound route additionally needs correlations of sufficiently large secret subfamilies; a typical-pair calculation does not supply that. An SQ lower bound would still exclude only the specified expectation-oracle algorithms.

**2. Boolean refutation degree — fully written small calculation; separation unresolved.** Again restrict q to prime. Stack m ring samples into mn scalar equations Cx≈b. For a proper residue set T of size D, form

    f_l(x)=∏_{e∈T}(b_l−C_l x−e),     x_j²−x_j=0.

Reduce modulo the Boolean equations. At degree d, span all multilinearly reduced monomial multiples with pre-reduction degree at most d; declare uniform only when that span contains 1. This precisely specified Boolean Macaulay procedure has polynomial matrix size for fixed d.

For target Gaussian noise, its planted soundness failure is at most mn·[1−Σ_{r∈T}W_{σ,q}(r)]. Conditioned on any C, null consistency has probability at most 2ⁿ(D/q)^{mn}. Thus the first comparison is the degree needed to refute ring-null versus iid-matrix-null instances, with planted controls, matched scalar counts, and explicit tails. Start n=4,8 and ternary bounded errors, recording that deliberate Gaussian-law change; only then test Gaussian instances.

**Refuted shortcut:** the original homogeneous forms (−C_l x)^D omit b entirely, so their ranks cannot distinguish planting conditioned on C. [Steiner, Definition 3.1 and §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07852v3), verifies the relevant algebraic objects, but does not prove favorable rank or degree for ring systems. Growing D and potentially exponentially many Boolean monomials are serious objections. A degree lower bound here would not establish cryptographic hardness.
