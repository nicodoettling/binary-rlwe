# SV-RS-DECODE-v1 author proof

Because p splits completely and the selected prime ideals are distinct, (p)J^-1 is the integral product of all unselected primes above p. Therefore a=p*lambda is in R, and its residue vector in R/pR has Fourier support contained in the selected t roots. Fourier inversion over F_p gives

    a_i = alpha^(-i) * f(alpha^(-2i)) modp,

for a polynomial f of degree<t, absorbing the invertible factor1/n into its coefficients. The n evaluation points x_i=alpha^(-2i) are distinct. For a cyclic shift of the roots, replace alpha^(-i) by the corresponding nonzero geometric coefficient factor. This is a generalized Reed–Solomon code of length n and dimension t; its Hamming minimum distance is n-t+1 because a nonzero polynomial of degree<t has at most t-1 distinct roots.

Parseval in the power-of-two coefficient basis gives ||w||_2^2=(1/n)*sum_sigma |sigma(w)|^2<=r^2. Let z be coefficientwise nearest-integer rounding of pT, with a fixed tie rule. If z_i differs from the integer a_i, then |p*w_i|>=1/2. Thus the number of such positions is at most4*p^2*||w||_2^2<=4*p^2*r^2. Reduction modulo p and multiplication by the nonzero column factors cannot increase the number of symbol errors. Put h=floor(4*p^2*r^2). The strict guard implies2h<n-t+1, hence n>=t+2h.

For completeness, error correction requires only an explicit linear system. On the scaled received symbols y_i solve for monic E of degree h and Q of degree<t+h satisfying Q(x_i)=y_i E(x_i) for all n positions. Such a solution exists: the product of the actual error-location factors has degree e<=h, can be padded by any monic factor of degree h-e, and Q=fE works. Any solution satisfies Q(x_i)=f(x_i)E(x_i) at the n-e correct positions. Since n-e>=n-h>=t+h and deg(Q-fE)<t+h, the polynomial Q-fE is zero. Thus polynomial division returns the unique f, and all codeword residues a_i modp are recovered. No probabilistic decoder or unproved finite-field factorization routine is used.

Choose the canonical residue c_i in{0,...,p-1} for each recovered coefficient. Then lambda_i=c_i/p+k_i for integers k_i. Since |w_i|<=||w||_2<=r<1/2, coefficientwise rounding of T_i-c_i/p recovers k_i uniquely. This gives exactly lambda under the promise. On an unpromised input the algorithm may return FAIL or any rational output; no efficient exact canonical-distance validation is needed for the pointwise promised correctness assertion.

We spell out ordinary-input acquisition. Compute e, the additive exponent of R/J, as the lcm of reduced denominators of the inverse integer basis. Under the theorem e=p. Check primality by deterministic trial division, whose cost is polynomial in the numeric p, and p=1 mod2n. Search F_p for an alpha satisfying alpha^n=-1; since n is a power of two this is exactly order2n. Evaluate each ideal basis column at the n roots to identify the subset of roots at which every element of J vanishes. Since pR is contained in J, ideals in the semisimple ring R/pR are exactly these root-vanishing subspaces, so the recovered subset determines J. Test cyclic intervals for every primitive2n-th root if necessary; there are n such roots and polynomially many tests. Recover t as the number of roots. Thus recognition needs no supplied factorization of J, and all operations are polynomial in n, numeric p and complete input lengths. The decoder then uses polynomial-size finite-field linear algebra, exact rational scaling and rounding, and polynomial division.

The radius substitution is algebraic. The known DD guards imply r<1/(4sqrt(n))<1/2. For the explicit dense family, t=Theta(n/log p) at fixed polynomial-value p, hence n-t=Theta(n). The improvement over the scalar guard is structural and pointwise. The example r=1/p demonstrates a nonempty mathematical improvement regime; compatibility with any specific reduction resource tuple is a separate parameter substitution, not inferred from this example.
