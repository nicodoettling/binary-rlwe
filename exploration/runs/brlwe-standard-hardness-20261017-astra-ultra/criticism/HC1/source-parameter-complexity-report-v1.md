## 2. Deriving the dispatcher cap without a new premise

The phrase "supplied all-input cap" in specification step4 need not be an additional user input. Here is a constructive cap procedure from the already fixed programs and Rec bound. It also explains precisely what the proof's constructible-polynomial-bound shorthand must mean.

First fix the quantum input grid and widths. Every target has the exact form Bv/H for 0<=v_i<H. Its coefficient magnitude is at most n max|B_ij|, while its numerator/denominator lengths are bounded from the ordinary B encoding and log2 H. These target bounds hold on every branch, with no BDD promise. Precompute the ordinary quotient/basis arithmetic and retain its actual setup cost. Rec always receives a canonical ring-sample encoding with the fixed public metadata; the supplied worst-case T_Rec applies to every such encoding and every tape, including the fixed valid dummy input.

Second bound all C8 state and numerical requests before simulation. There are exactly bounded outer loops, at most Q_* calls and c_max=kQ_* samples. Put

    tau_min = zeta/[16(c_max+1)^2].

The actual tau is never smaller. The walk has at most T_w+1 additions of its prescribed bounded dyadic step, so its coordinate magnitude and denominator lengths are bounded by the displayed Bbits and T_w, regardless of answers. Every logscale is a known rational in [0,M]. For a simple conservative magnitude bound, exp(t)<=2^(2M). The positive elliptical square root therefore has entries bounded by v0 2^(2M); enlarging by one covers its rational approximations. Its standard-Gaussian cutoff and every scalar Ks,Ls,Rcap are bounded by substituting tau_min into their monotone formulas and using the permitted upper rounding. For the standard sampler's own alpha use its smallest value tau_min/(64n); thus its output magnitude is at most its fixed cutoff, including its early-return and endpoint branches. Consequently every multiplier coefficient is bounded by

    n (v0 2^(2M)+1) (H_std+1) + a K_s + 3,

where H_std and K_s are the just-computed worst cutoffs. This is a value bound, whose binary length is polynomial in M and the parameter encodings; it is not a loop of that length in unary. The exact rational mean then has a finite value bound from this multiplier bound, the target bound and the walk-position bound. GridGaussian's support has at most 2R_G+1 points, but its search executes at most ceil(log2(2R_G+1))+2 iterations. None of these bounds depends on a successful decoder transcript.

Every tolerance for matrix/mean evaluation or Std is now bounded below by an explicit positive quantity: substitute tau_min, the just-bounded magnitudes and the fixed positive rational/algebraic inputs in the specified denominators. Their inverse binary lengths give a maximum requested precision. CDF evaluation is explicitly bounded by its J_eval and guard-bit formulas; exponential, logarithm, root and Fourier-constant evaluation use their fixed certified finite routines at this maximum argument length and precision. A fixed certified polynomial-time routine comes with a proved bound for these bounded requests; evaluating that bound is part of the construction. It is not an oracle returning the cost of an unknown favorable transcript. If one merely had black-box real evaluators or arbitrary uncapped code, this step would fail and the present theorem would not apply.

For clarity, a literal static bit-cost computation can replace every remaining unnamed polynomial. Associate to each program expression a worst numerator/denominator length: integer addition uses max(lengths)+1, multiplication uses their sum, rational addition/multiplication use the corresponding unreduced cross products, and division interchanges known nonzero numerator/denominator lengths. Exact normalization only reduces these bounds. Matrix products and negacyclic products are finite sums of these operations. Determinant and inverse-basis preprocessing can use ordinary exact algorithms with their fixed bounds. For each certified evaluator substitute its proved cost at the bounded argument/precision pair above. For sequencing add costs; for a conditional take the maximum branch cost and its comparison cost; for a loop multiply by its explicit bound. Update length envelopes along the loop, including possible unreduced denominator growth. No actual Gaussian support or exponentially large real magnitude is enumerated. This procedure yields a numerical upper bound T_0 on all non-Rec work of one complete C8/A/B program, plus final exact membership and output-coordinate conversion. It is conservative but independent of answers and coins. All length/loop recurrences are bounded using the finite program, rather than supplied as a new premise.

The B/A contribution is particularly direct: at most U_all elementary uniform draws, B_cap attempts each; at most 2kn L_+ scalar proposals; fixed dyadic acceptance precisions determined by ell, the supplied widths and rational centers; k exact ring products; and one T_Rec block. The B exact natural-log ceiling uses the separately bound polynomial bit algorithm, not an undecidable equality test. The finite group representatives have bounded ordinary encodings, so B's input lengths are bounded even on a wrong C8 orientation. Its transient grid samples may have larger numerators, already covered in the C8 length envelope before modular reduction.

Now use Q_* slots. Before each slot, run the non-call machine for T_0 simulated steps or until the next pending Rec invocation/termination, then freeze. Execute one T_Rec block on the pending valid input or the fixed dummy. Route or ignore the result, and continue. After the last slot run another T_0 non-call segment. Every genuine segment is a subcomputation of the full bounded program, so T_0 suffices. A conservative direct-machine bound before finite-alphabet simulation is

    (Q_*+1) T_0 + Q_* T_Rec + routing/copying overhead.

The routing term is explicitly bounded by the number of slots times the bounded state/input/output register lengths. Finite-alphabet simulation, if needed, has its usual fixed program-specific overhead; applying the same length/cost recursion supplies T_dec. This pads wrong orientations, inactive branches, early failures and variable stopping times. Fresh fixed coin blocks preserve the active program's law; unused bits and dummy outputs are retained and ignored. It therefore yields exactly Q_* executable Rec blocks per forward pass without enumerating branch-specific circuits. The finite one-slot control tests this semantic distinction, though it does not compile this enormous machine.

Thus the existing constructible-bound assertion is justified from the frozen interfaces and the supplied T_Rec. Specification step4's cap is fed by this construction. An optional user-supplied tighter numerical cap could improve resources, but is not needed for the theorem. The wording would benefit from calling it "derived"; no new theorem premise or frozen-version repair is required.

## 3. Actual circuit and host costs

After the finite target widths and the padded program are fixed, compile its actual Boolean circuit. S_dec counts all Boolean gates, r_dec all coin bits, and m=nb_u its output. The fourth-power bound in the theorem is a conservative unrolling bound for this fixed finite-alphabet machine with the padded T_dec, not for an arbitrary classical active path. A bit-machine unrolling can scan its bounded tape and state at each bounded step, and its construction/serialization is polynomial in the description and the full unrolling bound. This is why setup is separately charged.

The fixed gate set suffices for reversible evaluation of AND/XOR/NOT with work retained, coin superposition preparation, one output XOR and uncomputation. One forward plus one inverse computation yields 4S_dec+m+2r_dec and 2Q_* coherent recovery-code blocks. The large explicit G_0 covers F's nondecoder finite Machin/Taylor arithmetic, capped scalar/phase preparation, finite Fourier operations, measurements and ordinary output arithmetic. It does not stand in for a numerical S_dec. T_setup includes the actual host computation and serialization and cannot be inferred from S_dec alone. The theorem explicitly makes these distinctions.

No actual Rec program was supplied in the witness; therefore neither S_dec, r_dec nor T_setup has a concrete numerical value there. This is not a missing premise of the conditional theorem, but it prevents reading the witness's G_0 as the full executed cost. No quantum circuit, BDD walk, ideal Gaussian sampler or recovery call was executed in this review.
