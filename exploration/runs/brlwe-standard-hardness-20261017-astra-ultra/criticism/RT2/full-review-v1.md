## 1. Frozen scope and quantifiers

The rings are the entire integer rings of the power-two cyclotomic fields, in ordinary power coefficient bases. The dimensions n and m=tn are powers of two, n>=2, and t>=1. The coefficient norm, the field norm, and the ideal index are distinct quantities. Pi is normalized relative trace and selects coefficients with exponents divisible by t. The Gaussian is the normalized measure on the **entire** ideal, including zero; no truncation, rejection, or conditioning on nonzero output is implicit.

For the probabilistic claims, fix I, J, a nonzero a in C, the width W, and h before drawing the reference X. If a is random, every allowed a must satisfy the membership/norm assumptions, and the proof is applied conditionally on that a before fresh X is drawn. A separate multiplier failure probability is charged if those premises are not pointwise. An implemented source is compared with the complete reference law, including any failure tag; TV is applied only to the final bounded failure event.

The target does not assert a Gaussian output on I, the computability of a short a, an exact finite Gaussian sampler, an arbitrary-ideal sampling oracle, or a standard hardness reduction. These exclusions are material premises of the review.

## 2. Algebra: normalized trace and the exact multiplier module

Write S as the free R-module with basis 1,z,...,z^(t-1), where z^t=w. The minimal polynomial over K is T^t-w. Its conjugates give Pi(z^k)=0 unless t divides k; for the relevant basis products, Pi(1)=1 and Pi(z^t)=w. Thus the pairing matrix is the 1-by-1 block [1] and, for t>1, an anti-diagonal (t-1)-by-(t-1) block whose nonzero entries are w. Its determinant is a signed power of w, an R-unit. For t=1 the matrix is simply [1]. This verifies perfect R-duality without any integral division by t after taking the normalized trace. Equivalently, the different generated by t z^(t-1) is tS because z is a unit.

Certainly aF subset IS implies Pi(aF) subset I, since Pi is R-linear and Pi(IS)=I. Conversely, assume Pi(aF) subset I. For every f in F and s in S, sf remains in F, so Pi(afs) belongs to I. Expand af in the relative basis with coefficients in K. Pairing it with the basis elements gives a vector in I^t; multiplying by the inverse pairing matrix over R shows that every coordinate lies in I. Hence af belongs to IS. Since nonzero ideals of S are invertible, (IS:F)=IS F^(-1). This proves Claim 1, including fractional a, without assuming I principal. If the theorem's phrase “integral R-ideal” permits I=0, the same nondegeneracy argument gives a=0 and both sides are zero; the author's explicit nonzero-I restriction is harmless for the application.

For an odd prime I and prime J dividing IS, the power-two extension is unramified above I. In fact, divisibility alone suffices for the integrality of IS J^(-1); unramifiedness identifies it as the product of the other distinct primes. Norm multiplicativity and S being free of rank t over R give

    N_S(IS)=N_R(I)^t=M^t,   N_S(C)=M^t/D.

When there is only one inert prime above I, J=IS and C=S. If J and C both have index at most 2^(rho m), then M^t<=2^(2rho m), and taking m-th roots yields A<=2^(2rho). All directions of inclusion/index inequalities are correct. At rho=0 the odd-prime eligible-J premise is empty because D>1; no positive claim is extracted from that case.

## 3. Full Gaussian covariance and weighted second moment

For any full lattice F, centered symmetry gives E X=0. Absolute convergence permits completing the square and differentiating the moment generating function:

    E exp(<u,X>)
      = exp(W^2||u||^2/(4pi)) rho_W(F-W^2 u/(2pi))/rho_W(F).

Poisson summation gives the translated theta series as W^m/det(F) times a sum over the dual lattice with positive Gaussian coefficients and phases. Pairing v and -v turns phases into cosines, each at most one. Therefore rho_W(F-b)<=rho_W(F), with the entire normalizer retained, and the mgf is bounded by exp(W^2||u||^2/(4pi)). Along every real direction the functions agree at zero, have equal first derivative there, and the inequality of second derivatives yields

    Cov(X) <= (W^2/(2pi)) Id

in positive-semidefinite order. No coordinate independence, smoothing premise, or finite truncation is used.

The real coefficient matrix M_a of negacyclic multiplication has every row a signed permutation of a. Pi selects exactly n distinct rows. Hence

    ||Pi M_a||_F^2=n||a||^2,
    E||Pi(aX)||^2=tr((Pi M_a) Cov(X) (Pi M_a)^T)
                  <= n W^2||a||^2/(2pi).

This proves Claim 3's exact-reference moment. If a is random and independent of X, the displayed calculation is pointwise in a; an unconditional statement with random a has the corresponding expectation over ||a||^2. The theorem uses the pointwise interpretation, so no missing averaging factor is needed.

## 4. Proper-subspace estimate and the zero event

For an eligible ideal J of index D<=2^(rho m), Minkowski applied to expanding centered coefficient cubes and a discreteness limit supplies nonzero f in J with

    ||f||<=sqrt(m) D^(1/m)<=2^rho sqrt(m)<2sqrt(m).

The m rotations f,zf,...,z^(m-1)f form a real basis: multiplication by a nonzero field element is an invertible rational, hence real, coefficient map. Thus for any proper real linear subspace H, some rotation v lies outside H and has the same norm as f.

For x in J intersect H, the lines x+Z v are pairwise disjoint subsets of J. Put beta=<x,v>/||v||^2 and b=W/||v||. Direct completion of the square gives

    rho_W(x+Z v)/rho_W(x)
       = exp(pi ||v||^2 beta^2/W^2) rho_b(Z+beta)
       >= rho_b(Z+beta).

The factor is the **parallel-coordinate** correction, not the perpendicular correction as named in root's prose. The displayed inequality and resulting proof are unaffected; this is a wording issue only.

Scalar Poisson summation gives rho_b(Z+beta)>=b(1-2 sum_{k>=1}exp(-pi b^2 k^2)). For b>=1, the sum is at most e^(-pi)/(1-e^(-pi))<1/4, so the theta mass is at least b/2. Here W>=4sqrt(m) and ||v||<2sqrt(m), so b>2. Summing disjoint lines with their complete masses yields

    Pr[X in H] <= 2||v||/W <4sqrt(m)/W.

No post-selection law appears in this argument. With a nonzero a, multiplication by a is invertible and Pi has rank n, so H=ker(Pi M_a) is proper. This proves the stated zero bound. In particular, nonzero X alone is insufficient to imply nonzero Y when t>1.

## 5. Failure event, TV, and conditional laws

For a fixed valid multiplier, let Q be the exact full Gaussian law and P the implemented law, both on a common output space augmented with failure tags. The deterministic postprocessor checks ideal membership and returns failure on malformed or off-ideal inputs; on valid input it computes Y=Pi(aX). Its bad event is

    E = {tagged failure} union {Y=0} union {||Y||>B_a}.

Under Q the first event is empty. Markov applied to the exact-reference moment gives Q(||Y||>B_a)<=h for B_a=sqrt(n/(2pi h)) W||a||. The union bound gives Q(E)<=4sqrt(m)/W+h. TV then gives P(E)<=Q(E)+eta, once, and clipping at one proves the claim. No implemented second moment is inferred. No output distribution is renormalized onto good branches.

For random a, the natural filtration is G0=the supplied parameters and multiplier output (and any old randomness), followed by the fresh sample. Under the reference law, the conditional distribution of X given G0 must remain D_(J,W). A sufficient implemented premise is conditional TV<=eta for each allowed G0. If the multiplier fails its premises with probability beta, return failure there and add beta by a union bound. This avoids treating a successful multiplier-selection posterior as if it supplied fresh X automatically.

The frozen prose expressly requires this order. An unconditional marginal guarantee about X alone would not justify a random sample-dependent a. The finite negative control below records why the distinction is necessary.

## 6. Ideal scale, existence, and certificate

For a nonzero element b of an integral ideal H of degree d, its principal ideal is contained in H, so |N(b)|>=N(H). The power-two coefficient-to-embedding transform satisfies sum_sigma |sigma(b)|^2=d||b||^2. AM-GM gives |N(b)|<=||b||^d. Consequently ||b||>=N(H)^(1/d), including lambda_1(I)>=A and ||a||>=A/D^(1/m) for a in C.

Minkowski's cube argument in C supplies an element of norm at most sqrt(m) N(C)^(1/m)=sqrt(m) A/D^(1/m). This is an existence assertion; it does not select that vector efficiently. Substitution of the **supplied** upper bound on ||a|| gives

    ||Y||/lambda_1(I) <= B_a/A
       <= C0 sqrt(nm/(2pi h)) W/D^(1/m)

on the good event. Since D>=1, polynomial values of m,W,C0,h^(-1) imply a polynomial numerical certificate, provided the required source and multiplier costs/representations are separately polynomial. The normalization by A is valid even when A grows quickly, but it does not erase the bit lengths or construction cost of I, J, or a.
