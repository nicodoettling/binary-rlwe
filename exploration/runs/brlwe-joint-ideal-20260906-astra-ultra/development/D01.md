## Exact model and source law

Let `ell=ceil(log2 n)`, `R=F_q[X]/(X^n+1)`, and let Rts be its n CRT roots. Because n is a power of two and `q=1 mod 2n`, every root has exact multiplicative order 2n. For a d-subset T, the map `pi_T:R->F_q^d` evaluates on T in a fixed order. Its kernel J_T has dimension n-d and consists of vectors zero on T. Its annihilator I_T has dimension d and consists of vectors supported on T. In particular the complementary ideal is large while the active ideal is small; interchanging their dimensions would change the source and the extraction problem.

Candidate A samples T uniformly among all d-subsets, U with independent uniform **nonzero** coordinates on T and zero coordinates off T, and V uniformly from I_T including zero active coordinates. Thus multiplication by U identifies `R/J_T` with I_T exactly. U is not uniform over all of I_T. Changing U to that latter law changes the source marginal, produces a rank defect with positive probability, and requires the explicit source-law/rank penalties in F01.

The remaining candidate parameters are

    gamma=tau=ell, b=ell^2, sigma=2n*ell^3,
    sigma*n^c <= q <= n^K,

with fixed c>0. F is the actual F_impl law from F02: its finite coefficient table approximates D_ell conditioned on `[-b,b]`, and its support remains exactly within `[-b,b]^n`. An ideal-transcendental truncated-Gaussian variant is a separately identified source law. H is the actual F-dependent completion kernel from F02, sampled without S or bridge G. These source coins may depend on T and on each other exactly as their prescribed kernel permits. Their joint law conditional T is independent of S,G. The source public pair is `(U+F,V+H)`, whose exact decision hardness is a separate assumption.

Independently sample S with iid fair binary coefficients and G with iid wrapped D_ell coefficients, and put `Y=S+G` in R. For `Gamma=(J_T,U,F,H)`, its conditional independence gives

    Law(Gamma,Y,pi_T(S)|T)
      = Law(Gamma|T) tensor Law(Y,pi_T(S)|T).

Consequently (2) extends to `(T,Gamma,Y,pi_T(S))` versus `(T,Gamma,Y,Z_T)` for conditionally independent uniform Z_T. It keeps H; no averaging H away is used. Since (2) is uniform in T, this extension holds for any law of T independent of S,G. A change of support law would still change the separate source assumption, even though extraction itself remains valid.

## Exact collision and weighted root-count identity

Write W for the wrapped one-coordinate law of G. For every positive-mass fiber,

    Pr[Y_j=y]=(W(y)+W(y-1))/2,
    p_y=Pr[S_j=1|Y_j=y]=W(y-1)/(W(y)+W(y-1)),
    beta=4 E[p_Y(1-p_Y)].

The posterior bits are independent conditional the entire Y because the original coefficient pairs are independent. Draw S,S' independently from that SAME posterior given Y and set Delta=S-S'. After averaging Y, the pairs remain iid across coefficient positions and

    mu_beta(0)=1-beta/2,
    mu_beta(+1)=mu_beta(-1)=beta/4.                       (3)

This weighted ternary posterior-difference law is the coefficient law supplied to the affine-walk theorem. It is neither a uniform ternary replacement nor independent prior copies of S. Its precise L2 deficit is

    1-||mu_beta||_2^2=beta-3beta^2/8 >= 5beta/8.           (4)

For fixed T let `v_j=(r^j)_(r in T)`. Collision and finite Fourier orthogonality give the following equalities:

    C_T := q^d E_Y Coll(pi_T(S)|Y)
         = q^d Pr[pi_T(Delta)=0]
         = sum_(lambda in F_q^d)
             product_(j=0)^(n-1)
               (1-beta sin^2(pi*<lambda,v_j>/q)).         (5)

Angles can use any integer representative of the inner product. The lambda=0 term is 1 and all terms are nonnegative since `0<=beta<=1`. More intrinsically, `C_T-1` is the chi-square divergence of `(Y,pi_T(S))` from `(Y,Z_T)`. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore proves

    TV((Y,pi_T(S)),(Y,Z_T)) <= (1/2)sqrt(C_T-1).           (6)

For uniform T, let z(Delta) be the number of roots in Rts at which Delta vanishes, with z(0)=n. Counting d-subsets contained in that root set proves exactly

    M_d := E_T C_T
         = q^d E_Delta[binom(z(Delta),d)/binom(n,d)].      (7)

Here `M_d-1` is the chi-square divergence retaining T and Y, so its square root gives the analogous joint TV bound. This is the required factorial moment, not merely an entropy budget or an estimate of `E z(Delta)`.

For D_ell on the integers, let `Z_ell=sum_z exp(-pi*z^2/ell^2)`. Symmetry and unimodality imply that its TV distance from its unit translate is `1/Z_ell`; wrapping only decreases this TV. Also

    beta=2 sum_y W(y)W(y-1)/(W(y)+W(y-1))
        >= sum_y min(W(y),W(y-1))
        >= 1-1/Z_ell.                                   (8)

Since `Z_ell>=1+2 exp(-pi/ell^2)>2` for ell>=3, beta>1/2. On `[1/2,1]`, (4) is at least 13/32, which is strictly larger than rho=1/4 used below. Even the weaker lower bound 5beta/8 suffices.

## The primary joint-walk input and its exact specialization

The load-bearing source is Breuillard–Varju, *Irreducibility of random polynomials of large degree*, **arXiv:1810.13360v2**, Section 5, Definition 22 and Proposition 23, printed pages 23–24; its proof is in Section 5.3. This part is unconditional. The RH assumptions in the paper's main irreducibility results are not hypotheses of Proposition 23. Generic coordinates are distinct and nonzero; there is no inverse-pair or sign-pair exclusion. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.13360)

Specialize its notation to one prime: `M=1`, `p_1=q`, `m_1=D=d`, `Q=q`, and polynomial degree/walk index `n-1`. Thus `log(Q^D)=Lambda=d log q`, not `log(qd)`. Supply the iid law (3) for every coefficient, including the endpoints, which the proposition permits. Its support lies in `(-q/2,q/2)`. The Section 5 setup's prime bound q>=5 holds for every power-of-two n>=2 with `q=1 mod 2n`.

For `0<kappa<1/100`, call a coordinate admissible when it is not a root modulo q of a nonzero integer polynomial of degree at most `3Lambda` and Mahler measure at most `exp(kappa)`. Suppose every coordinate of an ordered tuple is admissible, and suppose

    ||mu_beta||_2^2 < 1-rho,
    Lambda >= max(kappa^(-1),rho^(-1)),
    n-1 >= C_BV*(kappa*rho)^(-1)*d*Lambda*(log Lambda)^3.  (9)

For any set A of distinct-coordinate admissible tuples, the proposition bounds the absolute error in their summed zero probabilities by

    E_BV=exp(-c_BV*rho*kappa*(n-1)
                    /(Lambda*(log Lambda)^2)).          (10)

For a singleton tuple, multiplying that absolute bound by q^d gives `0<=C_T-1<=q^d E_BV`. If A consists of all ordered distinct tuples from Rts, its size is `(n)_d=d! binom(n,d)`, and dividing before multiplying gives

    0<=M_d-1 <= (q^d/(n)_d) E_BV.                        (11)

The uniform singleton bound is the one used in (2); it deliberately pays the larger factor q^d. Proposition 23 controls evaluations using the same coefficients. Its proof uses independent blocks in the coefficient index, not independence between CRT output coordinates. Fourier frequencies with zero coordinates are handled by projecting to the corresponding smaller coordinate set in that proof. No multiplication of scalar mixing conclusions occurs here.

## Every CRT root is admissible: no support rejection

The needed primary Mahler estimate is Voutier, *An effective lower bound for the height of algebraic numbers*, Corollary 2, printed page 4: for a nonzero, non-root-of-unity algebraic number of degree m>=2,

    log M(alpha) > 2/(log(3m))^3.                        (12)

This is the directly inspected, effective refinement of Dobrowolski's bound used here. The original Dobrowolski 1979 scan was located but not legibly extracted, so it is not represented as independently inspected. [Voutier primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.3110)

Put `L=log n` and `kappa=L^(-3)`. For sufficiently large n,

    kappa<min(1/100,log 2,2/(log(3n))^3).                 (13)

Let P be a nonzero integer polynomial of degree e<n with `M(P)<=exp(kappa)`. Mahler measure is multiplicative and every nonzero integer factor has measure at least 1. A noncyclotomic irreducible factor of degree m>=2, other than X, would violate (12), because m<n. A linear integer irreducible factor other than X, X-1 or X+1 has measure at least 2. The integer content likewise has absolute value 1. Hence P is a signed product of powers of X and cyclotomic polynomials.

Fix a CRT root r, of order 2n. If P(r)=0, then some cyclotomic factor Phi_m satisfies `Phi_m(r)=0` and `phi(m)<=e<n`. If q divides m, then `phi(m)>=q-1>=2n`, a contradiction. If q does not divide m, `X^m-1` is squarefree modulo q. Its cyclotomic factorization then implies that roots of Phi_m have exact order m: a root of smaller order would be shared with a factor of `X^k-1` for some proper divisor k of m, contradicting squarefreeness. Therefore m=2n, but `phi(2n)=n`, another contradiction.

It follows that every CRT root is admissible whenever

    3d log q < n.                                       (14)

Thus all supports pass exactly, including supports containing r and -r or r and r^(-1). There is no high-probability event being silently treated as negligible. No rejection runtime, rejected mass, or change of support law is incurred. The strict degree bound matters: at degree n, `X^n+1=Phi_(2n)` itself has Mahler measure 1 and vanishes on every CRT root.

## Parameter verification and full joint conclusion

Take `rho=1/4`, `kappa=L^(-3)`, and d in (1). Since q is split, `q>=2n+1`, while `log q<=K L`. Thus

    L^3 <= dL <= Lambda <= KdL <= K sqrt(n)/L^3.

This proves `Lambda>=kappa^(-1)` and eventually `Lambda>=4`, while `log Lambda<=L` and (14) hold eventually. Equations (4) and (8) supply the strict L2 condition. The walk-length requirement in (9) is at most

    4 C_BV K d^2 L^7 <= 4 C_BV K n/L < n-1               (15)

for sufficiently large n. Write E for the positive exponent in (10). Using `n-1>=n/2` gives

    E >= c_BV*n/(8K*d*L^6).

The positive exponent introduced by q^d is at most KdL. Their ratio satisfies

    E/(KdL) >= c_BV*n/(8K^2*d^2*L^7)
             >= c_BV*L/(8K^2) -> infinity.               (16)

Eventually `E>=2KdL`, whence

    C_T-1 <= exp(-c_BV*n/(16K*d*L^6)),
    TV <= (1/2)exp(-c_BV*n/(32K*d*L^6)).                 (17)

This proves (2) with `c_K=c_BV/(32K)`, taking N_K large enough for all displayed thresholds. Its exponent is at least `c_K sqrt(n)/L^2`, so the error is negligible uniformly over the range. The constants depend only on fixed K and the absolute constants of the two primary results. No concrete practical threshold N_K is supplied.

Appending the conditional source kernel proves the full SC0 joint statement described above. Approximation of F,H or U by another independent kernel does not itself add an extraction loss: the same proof works for that actual kernel, although source correctness and hardness must use its actual law. If a whole-vector auxiliary sampler changes G by TV xi_G, the generic triangle argument pays at most `2xi_G`, one cost on each side of the extraction comparison. For an iid actual coefficient law W' within coefficient TV xi of W, one can instead apply the proof directly: the shift overlap gives `beta'>=1-1/Z_ell-2xi`, so sufficiently small xi still permits beta'>=1/2. Any separate noise-comparison cost of changing G remains in the bridge accounting.

## Finite-support reasoning checks and adversarial controls

These are exact symbolic checks, not new computational experiments.

1. **Same-Y posterior replicas.** For one bit, the probabilities of differences +1 and -1 are each `E[p_Y(1-p_Y)]=beta/4`; the remaining probability is `1-beta/2`. Independent prior replicas would instead force beta=1, losing the dependence on the actual auxiliary law. Equations (3)–(5) use the former distribution.
2. **Normalization.** A uniform output has collision q^(-d), so C_T=1. The zero frequency in (5) contributes exactly 1. Ordered distinct tuples multiply each support by d!, yielding `(n)_d` in (11); omitting this division is an error.
3. **Marginals do not determine factorial moments.** An abstract random variable z equal to n with probability 1/q and zero otherwise has `E z=n/q`, the uniform-looking first moment, but the expression in (7) is `q^(d-1)`. This is a logical moment control, not an assertion that this z law comes from our ternary polynomial. The exact E01 output provides an actual finite instance: for n=8,q=17,beta=1, single-root normalized collision is `4097/4096`, whereas the average two-root normalized collision is `21097/14336`. Marginal closeness does not settle joint extraction.
4. **Small finite cases.** The n=4,8,16 E01 examples are not in a verified finite regime of (9), (13)–(16), and do not test the unspecified asymptotic threshold. Their beta=1 and beta=1/2 diagnostics are not silently substituted for the Gaussian beta.
5. **Admissibility boundary.** Distinct sign or inverse pairs remain generic under the source definition. At degree n, Phi_(2n) defeats admissibility, which is why (14) is strict. For degrees below n the q-dividing-index case is explicitly excluded by phi(m)>=q-1.
6. **Auxiliary dependence.** Appending Gamma preserves the comparison because its conditional kernel is independent of S,G. Letting the source completion inspect bridge G or Y would invalidate that inference. F02's actual construction does not do so.
7. **Shared-K regression.** A uniform K can have every scalar marginal uniform while `(K,K)` is confined to a diagonal. Neither (5) nor Proposition 23 replaces a shared variable by independent copies. In particular this one-sample result cannot justify replacing repeated `US` masks by independent uniforms across several samples; D03 must track their common source secret and held-out-sample requirements separately.
