# B ideal-denominator primary sources and dependency notes

1. Pellet-Mary–Stehlé, *On the Hardness of the NTRU Problem*, [ePrint record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/821), [full PDF](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/821.pdf), [ASIACRYPT proceedings PDF](https://iacr.org/archive/asiacrypt2021/130900164/130900164.pdf). The record was accessible and identifies a 2026-09-03 correction to Theorem5.4. Direct PDF requests failed, but primary indexed text supplied Algorithm4.1, Lemmas4.2–4.3, Definitions5.1–5.2, the Theorem5.4 direction, and the paper's discussion distinguishing module recovery from vector recovery. The exact maps and unimported hypotheses are preserved in ps21-actual-map-v1.md. No positive theorem is imported from an unverified old version. The natural-lift denominator identity is an author derivation, not attributed to the paper.

2. Felderhoff–Pellet-Mary–Stehlé–Wallet, [author-hosted FPSW23 PDF](https://perso.jfelderhoff.fr/work/papers/FPSW23.pdf), 49-page version accessed through the browser. Corollary5.4 and Theorem6.2/Corollary6.3 specify the prime-ideal norm range, ideal-to-NTRU vector-search target, quantum/ERH and distributional conditions used in the comparison. The checked theorem does not state decision pseudorandomness of the fractional lift. In power-of-two cyclotomics its displayed norm range is beyond the simple low-index binary budget; that is a statement about the checked reduction, not a lower bound against all reductions.

3. Bootle–Brakerski–Peikert–Segev, *Order-LWE and the Hardness of Ring-LWE with Entropic Secrets*, [ASIACRYPT primary PDF](https://iacr.org/archive/asiacrypt2019/119210284/119210284.pdf), 30 pages. Definitions3.1–3.4 and the relevant Section6 reductions were checked for the actual joint challenge and advice. They concern noisy secret products with uniform public multipliers. They do not supply inverse-ideal Gaussian residue pseudorandomness by merely changing the source name. C's earlier published-width obstruction remains a separate packet.

4. Peikert–Regev–Stephens-Davidowitz, *Pseudorandomness of Ring-LWE for Any Ring and Modulus*, [author-hosted PDF](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/rlwe-anyring.pdf), ePrint2017/258. C owns the primary verification of Proposition4.4 and Lemma6.9. B does not claim successful direct retrieval of this PDF in the present subtask. The two propositions are used only in C's separately versioned hidden-center packet; the finite GP bridge and scalar samplers have self-contained proofs.

5. Bruna–Regev–Song–Tang, *Continuous LWE*, [primary arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.09595), 29 pages, fully opened through the browser. Printed pp.12–15, Definition3.7 and Lemma3.8, explicitly give the nonnegative-logscale hidden-center interface attributed to PRS17. The nearby proof shows why its starting-guess profile, rather than an unshifted radial profile, must satisfy the decay condition. B used this to audit C's v1 and confirm the shifted-center repair. The CLWE hardness theorem itself is not treated as hardness of our known-ideal periodic Gaussian law.

6. Lyubashevsky–Peikert–Regev, [primary ideal-LWE paper](https://cims.nyu.edu/~regev/papers/ideal-lwe.pdf), and the primary Toolkit references already preserved in ../references-v1.md. Uniform fractional-module ring-LWE transformations preserve a noisy-product challenge; they are not a theorem that short fractional-ideal Gaussian multipliers are pseudorandom. Full canonical and coefficient scales differ by sqrt(n) for the power-of-two ring considered here.

The scalar Gaussian, normalizer, finite-grid equivariance, and short-basis-existence arguments are new provisional mathematical derivations.
