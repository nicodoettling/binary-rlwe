# S12 — CONTROL: first lossiness obstruction and an exact-index test

**Finding 1 — fully written small calculation.** The first potentially fatal step is conditioning on the trapdoor and continuous precursor, before discretization or bounding the lattice index. Write $F=Z_0$, $HF=I+qK$, and $\Lambda=\mathbb Z^nF$. The old proof replaces the transcript by information sufficient to simulate it:

\[
U=(SH+EF^{-1})\bmod q.
\]

Given $F,U$, compute $V=UF$, $W=\operatorname{center}_q(V)$, and $J=(V-W)/q$. On $\|S+E\|_\infty<q/2$, $J=SK-kF$ for some integer $k$, so

\[
S\equiv-qJ\pmod\Lambda.
\]

Thus the precursor already reveals the secret's lattice coset. This is necessary leakage **of the precursor**, while $F,U$ are analyst side information. The public transcript need not reveal either.

With coefficient continuous width $t_1$, no-wrap failure is at most $2n\exp[-\pi(q/2-1)^2/t_1^2]$. The decomposition uses $t_1=\sigma_{0,\mathrm{coeff}}/L$, where $L$ upper-bounds the concatenated rational-ratio norm. The published bound can be enlarged to $L\ge1$; consequently the reference polynomial gap $q/\sigma_{0,\mathrm{coeff}}\ge n^c$ makes failure negligible. For the actual norm instead, the explicit condition is $qL/\sigma_{0,\mathrm{coeff}}\gg\sqrt{\log n}$.

**Source-verified result plus deduction:** on the 2020 inverse-norm good event, $\sigma_{\min}(F)\ge\gamma/(24\sqrt n)$. If $\gamma>24n$, no nonzero binary difference can belong to $\Lambda$: its norm is at most $\sqrt n$, whereas every nonzero lattice vector has larger norm. Hence the binary quotient is injective and precursor residual entropy is negligible. The published requirement $\gamma\ge8n\eta_\epsilon(R)$ implies this asymptotically because canonical geometry scales coefficient geometry by $\sqrt n$. This deduction concerns the specified good event, not every denominator. [2020 paper, Theorem 5.7 and Lemmas 6.5–6.8, pp.18–22](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1185.pdf).

The rectangular Module-NTRU continuation improves parameters but retains this first step: Appendix B.3.5 Eq. (7), before lattice replacement Eq. (8) and index charge Eq. (11). Ordinary LWE instead overconditions on a freely sized projection $SB$. [2022 paper, pp.41–42](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/245.pdf); [ordinary theorem, pp.16–18](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/119.pdf).

**Serious objection / unresolved implication:** precursor recoverability is no public-transcript attack. Indeed $F=Z=2I$ gives $A=I$: the precursor recovers binary $S$, while the transcript $S+E\bmod q$, with the reference discrete Gaussian of width $n$, retains $n-O(1)$ bits. The exact calculation is in [support](S12/calculations.md). A repair must analyze information after the simulation map or change the trapdoor distribution; tightening the final determinant bound alone does not repair the standard good event.

**Finding 2 — fully written small calculation; simple replacement refuted.** For $f=1+X$, the quotient $R/(f)\simeq\mathbb F_2$ leaks only coefficient parity: $n-1$ bits remain. Its exact determinant costs one bit, versus $n\log_2\sigma_{\max}(M_f)=n-O(1/n)$. Thus exact-index accounting can mathematically change the feasible entropy regime.

But $2/f=1-X+\cdots-X^{n-1}\in R$. For a ratio $a=z/f\bmod q$ whose rational multiplication norm is at most $L$, every coefficient of $\operatorname{center}_q(2a)$ has absolute value at most $2L$, provided $2L<q/2$. Uniform $a$ passes this public test with probability at most $((4L+1)/q)^n$. One ring sample therefore distinguishes this candidate in the desired gap. **Next cheap diagnostic:** compute the quotient exponent $\kappa$ for any proposed denominator and test $\kappa L\ll q$ before proposing a hardness assumption.
