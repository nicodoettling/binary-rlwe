# Explicit complementary multiplier test v1

Root-authored provisional calculation, 2026-10-18. This tests an executable candidate for the missing multiplier; it does not assert a standard hardness result or a general obstruction.

Fix the RT1 tower R subset S, degrees n,m=tn, odd prime ideal I above rational p, a supplied prime J above I, and C=IS J^(-1). Assume J is supplied as (p,g(z)) with monic irreducible factor g of X^m+1 modulo p. Compute the monic quotient H=(X^m+1)/g in F_p[X] and lift its coefficients to centered integers. Then a=H(z) is nonzero because deg H<m. Since X^m+1 is squarefree modulo odd p, a belongs to every prime over p other than J, hence to (p)J^(-1), which is contained in IS J^(-1)=C. This is an ordinary exact polynomial division, with no latent sampler or short-vector advice.

The elementary height certificate is ||a||<=(p/2)*sqrt(m-deg(g)+1). It is polynomial in the numerical VALUE p, not log p. In fact a=p already belongs to C and has the better norm certificate p. Taking the latter in the weighted-trace radius gives a factor at most sqrt(n/(2pi h))*W*p/A, A=N(I)^(1/n). But the public vector p itself is in I and already gives approximation certificate p/A, without any binary-recovery algorithm or Gaussian sampler. Consequently this available uniform-height certificate produces no new hard-family solver. If p/A is polynomial, the ideal family is already easy at that approximation scale by this public vector. A sharper instance-specific short H could help, but proving/constructing such height at the required scale is exactly an additional unresolved obligation. This is not a lower bound on H or actual output lengths.

Both endpoints of the fixed weighted map T_a(x)=Pi(ax): its full planted law at y in L_a=T_a(J) is sum_(x in J:T_a(x)=y) exp(-pi||x||^2/W^2)/rho_W(J). It is not asserted Gaussian on I or even full-support on I. A uniform input on J/qJ maps exactly to uniform L_a/qL_a. Its further image in I/qI is uniform on (L_a+qI)/qI, which need not be all I/qI. Thus this construction provides an ideal-vector extraction condition, not an indistinguishability reduction with an automatically uniform target comparator. For a=p, L_a=p Pi(J); even when Pi(J)=R the support lattice is pR, generally a proper subideal of I.

Freshness: a is computed from public ideals before X. Choosing a after X, for instance a proportional to conjugate(X), requires a different joint-law argument; RT2 preserved an exact counterexample to applying the independent multiplier moment in that situation. No experiment is required for this symbolic test, and no new source oracle has been supplied.
