1. Acquisition algorithm

The alphabet of possible slots consists of zero and all a*X^i with
0<=i<n and 1<=|a|<=B, so it has 1+2nB entries. Enumerate ordered h-tuples
of slots. Sum each tuple and reject zero or any result with a coefficient
outside [-B,B] or with more than h nonzero coordinates. Every qualifying
polynomial occurs: place each of its nonzero monomials in one slot and pad
with zeros. The total tuple count is exactly (1+2nB)^h, an upper bound on
the number of membership tests even though many tuples duplicate a result.

Membership in J is precisely A^{-1}f in Z^n. Compute the rational inverse
once, then use exact integer/rational operations to test this condition.
Input matrix entries may be large or the basis badly conditioned; their
full encoding lengths are explicitly charged. Fraction-free linear
algebra bounds all intermediate/output bit lengths polynomially in those
lengths, n, and the candidate coefficient bit lengths. Thus the displayed
enumeration times a polynomial is valid. For fixed h and polynomial
numeric B the entire search is polynomial. There is no factoring, hidden
short-member advice, or need for J to be principal.

2. Decoding guard

Since n is a power of two, X^n+1 is irreducible over Q. A nonzero f is
therefore invertible in the field Q[X]/(X^n+1). By f in J and lambda in
J^{-1}, the coefficients of f*lambda are integers. Each coefficient of
f*w is a signed inner product of a coefficient permutation of f with w,
so its absolute value is at most ||f||_coef,2 ||w||_coef,2.
Parseval gives ||w||_coef,2<=||w||_can,infty<=r. Consequently
  2r ||f||_coef,2<1
ensures every coefficient of f*T is strictly within 1/2 of the correct
integer coefficient of f*lambda. Coefficient rounding then division by f
recovers lambda exactly. The promise ||f||^2<=hB^2 proves the sufficient
guard 4r^2 hB^2<1, and the sharper exact norm test is valid per candidate.
Strict inequality eliminates all rounding ties. Exact rational division
by f has polynomial bit complexity in n, coefficient/input encodings, and
the charged output lengths. No Gaussian oracle or approximate arithmetic
is present.

3. Lucas quotient proof

Let phi be the residue class of X in O=Z[X]/(X^2-X-1). This is a free
rank-two abelian group with basis 1,phi. It has phi^2=phi+1 and
phi*(phi-1)=1, so phi is a unit. Its conjugate is psi=1-phi=-phi^{-1}.
The recurrence and initial conditions show
  L_j=phi^j+psi^j.
For n>=4 a power of two, m=n/2 is even. Hence psi^m=phi^{-m}, and
  phi^n+1=phi^{2m}+1=(phi^m+phi^{-m})phi^m=L_m phi^m.
Since phi^m is a unit, the principal ideals (phi^n+1) and (L_m) in O are
identical. Therefore
  R/(X^2-X-1)R
    = Z[X]/(X^n+1,X^2-X-1)
    = O/(phi^n+1)O
    = O/L_m O
has additive group (Z/L_m Z)^2. This proves both the exact additive
exponent L_m and exact ideal norm L_m^2.

In the real embedding phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2>1. Since m is even,
L_m=phi^m+phi^{-m}; it and its square grow exponentially in n. Moreover
  log2(L_m^2)=n log2(phi)+2 log2(1+phi^{-n}).
For every fixed rho>log2(phi), the second term is eventually less than
n(rho-log2(phi)), proving N(J_n)<=2^(rho n). The stated upper restriction
rho<1 is compatible with log2(phi)<1.
