# B-JL-v2 proposed proof

Status **PROVISIONAL**. Applies only to joint-lattice-v2-theorem.md, frozen 2026-10-16 21:23 UTC. No nonauthor or blind certification exists.

## 1. Cyclotomic norm gate

For integer z in O, power-of-two Parseval gives sum_sigma |sigma(z)|^2=n||coeff(z)||_2^2. If z is nonzero, its field norm is a nonzero integer and AM-GM gives

  1 <= |N(z)| <= ||coeff(z)||_2^n.

If z vanishes modulo q at s of the distinct CRT roots, the s distinct prime ideals above q divide (z), so q^s divides N(z). This also follows by reducing the product of the n conjugate factors in the split algebra, with valuation multiplicities justified by the unramified factorization of q. Thus

  q^(s/n) <= ||coeff(z)||_2.

This is a deterministic necessary condition, not a decoding algorithm.

If nonzero h has coefficient bound C with sqrt(n)C<sqrt(q), it cannot vanish at n/2 or more roots. It is nonzero on strictly more than n/2 roots. In particular a nonzero h-pair has union CRT support greater than n/2. For H-fixed h, that support is a union of H-orbits. H acts freely on the root list, so every orbit has size |H| and there are r_H=[G:H] of them. Consequently the union support contains more than r_H/2 orbits.

## 2. Public witness implies hidden cancellation on the coefficient-tail event

Let F_1,F_2 have all coefficients in [-b,b]. For a proposed public witness choose y to be the centered coefficient representative of h_1 A_1+h_2 A_2. In O define

  z=y-h_1 F_1-h_2 F_2.

Every coefficient of a negacyclic product h_i F_i is a signed sum of n products of magnitudes at most Cb. Hence ||z||_2<=sqrt(n)(B+2nCb). Modulo q, z=h_1 U_1+h_2 U_2, which is zero outside T. If z were nonzero, the norm gate with s>=n-d would give ||z||_2>=q^(1-d/n), contradicting the strict hypothesis. Thus z=0 as an integer ring element, and necessarily h_1 U_1+h_2 U_2=0 modulo q.

This implication is pointwise and allows the witness to depend on all public inputs, including both errors through A. It does not supply a public cancellation oracle or reveal T or F. If an algorithm's output lies outside the magnitude or fixed-field restrictions, this implication cannot be used to classify that algorithm.

For D_ell and integer b>0,

  Pr[|F|>b] <= (ell/(pi b)) exp(-pi b^2/ell^2).

Indeed the omitted positive sum is bounded by the integral from b to infinity; the two-sided Gaussian integral is at most ell^2 exp(-pi b^2/ell^2)/(pi b); and Poisson summation gives Z_ell>=ell. A union over 2n coefficients gives eta_F. No independence is needed by this tail union, although SC-v1 supplies it.

## 3. Occupancy uniformly over candidate supports and fields

Fix H and an orbit union S containing at least half the roots, and let N_S=|T intersect S|. For 0<t<1 the exact generating function is

  E[t^N_S] = e_d(w_1,...,w_n)/C(n,d),

where w_i=t on S and 1 elsewhere. For nonnegative w_i of fixed sum, the elementary symmetric polynomial e_d is at most C(n,d)(sum_i w_i/n)^d. An elementary proof repeatedly replaces two coordinates by their average: terms containing neither are unchanged; terms containing exactly one depend only on their sum; terms containing both have nonnegative coefficient and increase with their product. By compactness a maximizer exists, and averaging unequal coordinates with a positive e_(d-2) coefficient increases the value; boundary degeneracies follow by adding a positive epsilon and taking its limit. Thus the all-equal vector is a maximizer. This supplies the entire inequality used here.

Since |S|/n>=1/2, E[t^N_S]<=(1/2+t/2)^d. Set t=1/3 and apply Markov to t^N_S:

  Pr[N_S<=d/4] <= 3^(d/4)(2/3)^d = a^d,
  a=2/3^(3/4)<1.

There are at most 2^r_H candidate orbit unions. Union over all N_G subgroups of index <=r gives probability at most N_G 2^r a^d that any qualifying union contains <=floor(d/4) active roots. The good event depends on T alone and gives N_S>=L=floor(d/4)+1 for every qualifying S simultaneously. It does not condition on U or on a success/failure transcript.

The subgroup count N_G<=n^ell follows because any subgroup of a group of order 2^ell can be generated by at most ell elements: adding an element outside the subgroup generated so far at least doubles its order. Padding with identities embeds a generating list into G^ell, which has n^ell lists. This is only an upper bound and does not require enumerating them.

## 4. Exact full-product amplitude law and union bound

Now fix any T in the good occupancy event. Conditional on that exact T, the random pairs (U_1(t),U_2(t)) at the d active roots are independent uniform F_q^2. Zero pairs and one-zero pairs are included. Fix H, an orbit union S, and, for every orbit in S, one projective nonzero multiplier direction [a_j:b_j] in P^1(F_q). Any nonzero scalar representative defines the same cancellation equation.

For each root t in T intersect S, a_j U_1(t)+b_j U_2(t)=0 has probability exactly 1/q. The entire family of equations acts on disjoint independent F_q^2 variables, so its full joint probability is exactly q^(-N_S)<=q^(-L). This is a product under a declared conditional product law, with every equation fixed before those amplitudes are drawn. It is not a product of adaptive posterior marginals.

There are q+1 projective directions per orbit, at most (q+1)^r for a whole direction assignment, and at most 2^r unions. Union over H,S and directions gives at most N_G 2^r (q+1)^r q^(-L). Any actual nonzero H-fixed h-pair supplies one such assignment on its union support, which by Section 1 includes more than half the orbits. This covers its adaptive choice after seeing A, U, F or even all latent data. Together with the bad-T event, exact hidden cancellation has probability at most eta_rel. Section 2 then proves planted Pr[W]<=eta_rel+eta_F.

CP-1 scope check: the old cached-prefix model has matching probability 21/2000=0.0105>0.01, while the initial residual bound is gamma=0.1 and the posterior residual incidence is 21/221<gamma; local restored independence cannot justify that stage product. This proof never applies such an inference. Its only amplitude product conditions on the complete fixed T (or averages separately over T in the good event), and all root-coordinate equations are fixed under an explicit independent uniform product measure before a union over possible assignments. There is no latent cache test, adaptive selection, or support restriction involving amplitude values in that product.

## 5. Uniform endpoint and integer multiplier count

Fix H and a permitted nonzero h-pair. Under independent uniform A_1,A_2, their CRT pairs are independent uniform F_q^2 at all n roots. Thus Y=h_1 A_1+h_2 A_2 is uniform in the CRT coordinate subspace I_S supported on the union S of the h-pair, of dimension s=|S|>n/2.

The coefficient representation of this s-dimensional subspace has some s coordinate projections of rank s, because its n-by-s basis matrix has rank s. Consequently those s coefficient coordinates of uniform Y are independent uniform F_q. The event that all n centered coefficients lie in [-B,B] implies that these s do, and has probability at most rho^s<=rho^(n/2). This pivot-coordinate argument is essential: the looser box-cardinality bound would be insufficient for some permitted C,B. The choice of pivot coordinates is proof-only and depends on the fixed h-pair, not on A.

The rational H-fixed subspace has dimension r_H. Select r_H coefficient coordinates on which restriction is injective. An integer vector in this subspace with every coefficient in [-C,C] is determined by its integer values on those coordinates, so there are at most (2C+1)^r_H such vectors. Hence at most (2C+1)^(2r) candidate pairs per H. The zero pair is omitted. The first strict gate implies C<q, so a nonzero permitted integer pair is not the zero pair modulo q. Union over pairs and H proves eta_unif. No Gaussian approximation or replacement is used at this endpoint.

## 6. Effective asymptotic specialization

For both baseline d families, d/log^2 n tends to infinity and d<=n/(64ell) eventually. Put r=o(d), C=n, b=ell^2 and B=2n^2 ell^2. Then sqrt(n)C=n^(3/2)<sqrt(q) from q>=2n^3+1. Also

  sqrt(n)(B+2nCb)=4n^(5/2)ell^2,
  q^(1-d/n)>n^3

for large n: substitute q>=2n^3 and d/n<=1/(64ell), whose exponent loss is at most the constant factor 2^(1/16). Hence the strict gate holds eventually because 4ell^2<sqrt(n). The box gate holds as B/q=O(ell^2/n).

Writing N_G<=exp(O(log^2 n)), the two eta_rel summands are exp(-Omega(d)) and exp(-Omega(d log q)) once r=o(d). They are negligible because d/log^2 n tends to infinity. eta_F=2n exp(-pi ell^2)/(pi ell) is negligible. Finally rho=O(ell^2/n), while log[N_G(2n+1)^(2r)]=O(log^2 n)+O(r log n)=o(n)+O(log^2 n); therefore eta_unif=exp(-Omega(n log n)). These are asymptotic statements with parameters fixed before the adversary; no practical threshold is asserted.

## 7. Low-degree supplement

Fix T. For a nonzero polynomial pair (h_1,h_2) of degrees <k over F_q, write h_i=g f_i where g is its monic gcd, deg g=j in [0,k-1], and gcd(f_1,f_2)=1. This includes one h_i=0, taking the monic gcd of the nonzero member. The pair vanishes jointly at at most j roots, because those roots are roots of g. Therefore its cancellation probability on T is at most q^(-(d-j)).

There are q^j monic degree-j polynomials g. There are at most q^(2(k-j))-1 nonzero residual pairs of degree <k-j; only coprime pairs actually occur, so ignoring that requirement upper bounds their number. Multiplying a residual pair by a common nonzero scalar gives the same cancellation equations and acts freely, so division by q-1 counts at most the number of projective residual classes. For each j, union probability is at most

  q^j (q^(2(k-j))-1)/(q-1) * q^(-(d-j))
   = (q^(2k)-q^(2j)) q^(-d)/(q-1).

Summing j=0,...,k-1 proves <=k q^(2k-d)/(q-1). This count is exact in its probability model and valid for every T; it does not assume active values. A public adaptive pair is covered by the existence union. The public short-output implication replaces n summands per product by k summands, giving the stated 2kCb gate.
